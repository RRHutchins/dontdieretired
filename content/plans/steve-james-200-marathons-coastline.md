## Why this works

Steve James ran 200 marathons in 200 days around Britain's coast in 2025, and University of Exeter researchers found no sign of harm in his fortnightly blood tests. Nobody needs to copy the distance. What is worth copying is the habit underneath it: easy effort, done again the next day, with strength work to keep the legs able to take it.

This plan builds three things in order: running without stopping, then running two days in a row, then running on rough, hilly paths several days running. The NHS (the UK health service) recommends at least 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week, such as running, or 150 minutes of moderate activity, plus strengthening activities on at least two days. Both tracks below meet that.

## Before you start

- **Check with a GP (family doctor) first** if you have not exercised for some time, have a heart condition, or have had chest pain, dizziness or breathlessness on exertion. The NHS advises speaking to your GP first if you have not exercised for some time.
- **Shoes you have already run in.** For coast and trail paths, shoes with grip help on mud and grass.
- **Run at a talking pace.** Most of this plan should feel easy enough to speak in sentences.
- **On coast paths:** check the tide and the weather, keep away from cliff edges, carry water, food, a waterproof and a charged phone, and tell someone your route.
- **Strength twice a week, every week:** squats or sit-to-stands, step-ups, calf raises and a plank, two or three sets each.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 (getting going) | Follow the first weeks of Couch to 5K: short spells of running between walks. | 3 sessions, plus 2 strength | Repeat a week if it felt too hard. That is normal. |
| 1 to 3 (already fit) | Easy runs of 40 to 60 minutes; one long run of 75 minutes, rising to 90. | 4 runs, 2 strength | Note your easy pace on the flat. |
| 4 (both) | Easier week: shorter sessions, same strength. | — | Let your legs catch up. |
| 5 to 7 (getting going) | Carry on with Couch to 5K until you can run 20 minutes without stopping. | 3 sessions, 2 strength | Run on grass or a park path once a week. |
| 5 to 7 (already fit) | Back-to-back weekend: 90 minutes on Saturday, 60 on Sunday, then 105 and 75. | Weekly, plus 2 easy runs and 2 strength | Practise eating and drinking on the Saturday run. |
| 8 (both) | Easier week. Check your shoes and kit; replace what rubbed. | — | James wore out seven pairs of trainers. |
| 9 to 11 (getting going) | Run 25, then 30 minutes without stopping. Once a week, run 20 minutes the day after a run. | 3 sessions, 2 strength | The second day should feel slow. Let it. |
| 9 to 11 (already fit) | Back-to-back on hilly trail or coast path: 13 miles (21 km) then 8 miles (13 km), with 1,500 ft (450 m) or more of climbing on the first day. | Weekly, plus 2 easy runs and 2 strength | Walk the steep climbs; it saves your legs for the second day. |
| 12 (getting going) | Run 30 minutes on Saturday and 30 on Sunday on a path you enjoy. | Once | Your first back-to-back weekend. |
| 12 (already fit) | Three consecutive days of 10 to 13 miles (16 to 21 km) on a coast or trail path. | Once | Plan where you will sleep and how you will get back. |

## If it goes wrong

- **Sore for more than two days.** Take an extra easy day or repeat the week. Sharp pain, or pain that changes how you run, means stop and get it looked at.
- **Blisters.** Deal with a hot spot when you first feel it: stop, dry the foot, plaster it.
- **The second day feels awful.** That is the training. Slow down, walk the hills, and finish shorter if you need to.
- **A missed week.** Pick up where you left off. Do not try to catch up.

## Track it

After each run, note the time, the distance, the climbing, and how your legs felt the next morning. On back-to-back weekends, note what you ate and drank. Those notes tell you when you are ready to add more.

## What next

If you are getting going, finish Couch to 5K and try a 5 km event; our [Couch to 5K guide](/list/couch-to-5k/) explains the plan. If you already run, look at a multi-day section of a coast path, a hilly trail half marathon or a first ultra. Steve James built to his 200 days over a lifetime of challenges, from Ten Tors as a schoolboy to cycling round the world in 2019. Each one started with a single day.
