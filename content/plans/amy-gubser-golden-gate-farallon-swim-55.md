## Why this works

Amy Appelhans Gubser did not start with 29.7 miles. She started with a relay, then five years of planning, cold sessions at Aquatic Park and a crew that studied the currents for three months. The method is the lesson: build distance where it is safe and boring, add cold and open water in small doses, and never do the hard part alone.

A pool gives you a wall every 25 metres and a clock on the wall, so progress is easy to measure. Open water takes away the wall, the lines and the warmth, so you add those challenges one at a time. Three or four swims a week also covers the 150 to 300 minutes of weekly activity the World Health Organization recommends.

## Before you start

- **Check with your GP (family doctor)** before cold-water swimming if you have a heart condition, high blood pressure or asthma, or if you have not exercised for a while.
- **You should be able to swim 100 metres** without stopping, any stroke, before starting the "getting going" track. If you cannot yet, take adult lessons first; our [swimming and cycling guide](/move/swimming-and-cycling-after-50/) explains how to find them.
- **Kit:** costume, goggles, a bright silicone cap, a tow float for open water. A wetsuit is sensible for most people in UK waters and in the northern US; Gubser's swimsuit-only swim followed years of acclimatisation.
- **Where:** a supervised open-water venue with safety cover, or a lifeguarded beach. Ask your local masters club where its members swim.
- **A partner.** Do not skip this one.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 (getting going) | Pool: 400 m total as 8 × 50 m, resting 30 seconds between each. | 2 swims a week | Any stroke. Finish feeling you could do more. |
| 1–2 (already swimming) | Pool: 2 km a session, including 4 × 400 m at steady pace. | 3 swims a week | Note your 400 m time. It is your benchmark. |
| 3–4 (getting going) | Pool: 600 m total as 6 × 100 m. | 2–3 swims a week | Breathe out under water; most tiredness is breath-holding. |
| 3–4 (already swimming) | Pool: 2.5 km a session; one continuous 1.5 km swim each week. | 3–4 swims a week | Practise sighting: lift your eyes forward every six strokes. |
| 5–6 (getting going) | Pool: 800 m, including one continuous 400 m. First open-water dip: 5–10 minutes, close to the bank. | 2 pool, 1 open water | Get in slowly. Stay within your depth the first time. |
| 5–6 (already swimming) | Pool: 3 km a session. Open water: 20–30 minutes continuous. | 3 pool, 1 open water | Swim next to someone to get used to company and chop. |
| 7–9 (getting going) | Pool: 1,000–1,200 m, including a continuous 800 m. Open water: build to 400 m, then 800 m. | 2 pool, 1 open water | Add a few minutes per open-water swim, no more. |
| 7–9 (already swimming) | Pool: one 3 km continuous swim a week plus two interval sessions. Open water: build to 3 km. | 3 pool, 1–2 open water | Practise feeding: a drink at 40 minutes without touching the bottom. |
| 10–11 (getting going) | Pool: a continuous 1,600 m. Open water: 1,200 m. | 2 pool, 1 open water | You have now swum the mile indoors. The rest is confidence. |
| 10–11 (already swimming) | Open water: 4 km once; pool sessions drop to 2 km, relaxed. | 2 pool, 1 open water | Enter a 5 km event if you have not already. |
| 12 | Getting going: a continuous open-water mile. Already swimming: your 5 km event. | Once, with two easy swims earlier in the week | Tell your partner the plan and agree a signal for "I want to stop". |

## If it goes wrong

- **You gasp when you get in.** That is the body's normal reaction to cold. Enter slowly, keep your face out until your breathing settles, and wear a wetsuit.
- **You panic without the wall.** Roll onto your back, hold your tow float and breathe. Swim parallel to the bank at standing depth until it passes.
- **Sore shoulders.** Drop the distance by a third for a week and book one technique session with a coach.
- **You miss a week.** Repeat the last week you completed.
- **The water is too cold by week 12.** Do the mile in the pool and pencil in open water for spring.

## What next

- Join a masters club and swim with it weekly: U.S. Masters Swimming in the US, a Swim England masters club in the UK. Coaching and company are what keep people at it.
- Getting going? Enter an organised one-mile open-water event next season. Already swimming? Look at a 10 km (6.2-mile) event, the marathon distance of the sport.
