## Why this works

Dave Fox, 70, and Judy Fox, 67, of Grand Junction, Colorado, walked 490 miles from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Santiago de Compostela in 2022, and have gone back for other Camino routes since, Beacon Senior News reported. Their preparation is simple. They start three months ahead, lengthen their hikes to 12 miles, put on the actual packs a month before they go, and in the final weeks walk three or four times a week, adding weight as they go.

The point is not one long walk. As Judy put it, the hard part is "getting up the next day and doing it again." So this plan builds distance first, then the pack, then consecutive days, which is what a Camino really asks of you.

## Before you start

- **Check with your GP (family doctor) first** if you have a heart, lung or joint condition, or have not been active for a while.
- **Know your brisk pace.** The NHS describes brisk walking as about 3 mph (5 km/h): you can still talk but could not sing.
- **Shoes and socks.** Walk in what you will travel in. The Foxes use trail runners and test every piece of kit, socks included, before they go. Shoes should be comfortable, supportive and not cause blisters, the NHS says.
- **A pack.** Use the one you will carry. The Foxes use 30 and 22 litre packs.
- **Order a credential early** if you are going. American Pilgrims on the Camino asks non-members for a $5 minimum donation and says to allow four weeks for US addresses and six for others; most starting points also have them. UK walkers can start with the Confraternity of St James, linked from our [Camino activity page](/list/walk-a-camino/).

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 (getting going) | Brisk walks of 30 to 45 minutes, plus one longer walk of 3 miles (5 km) | 3 times a week | Talk-but-not-sing pace. Note any rubbing on your feet |
| 1 to 2 (already walking) | Walks of 5 to 6 miles (8 to 10 km), one on hilly ground | 3 times a week | Start in the shoes and socks you will travel in |
| 3 to 5 (getting going) | Lengthen the longest walk by about a mile a week, to 6 miles (10 km) | 3 times a week | Keep the other two walks short and easy |
| 3 to 5 (already walking) | Build the longest walk to 10 miles (16 km) | 3 times a week | Eat and drink on the move, as you will in Spain |
| 6 to 8 (getting going) | Longest walk to 8 miles (13 km); add a second walk of 4 miles the next day | 3 times a week | The back-to-back pair is the key session |
| 6 to 8 (already walking) | Longest walk to 12 miles (19 km), the Foxes' training distance | 3 to 4 times a week | Practise the umbrella or waterproof you will carry |
| 9 to 10 (both) | Put on the real pack, light at first, and add weight each week | 3 to 4 times a week | Weigh the pack. Take out anything you have not used |
| 11 (getting going) | Two days running of 8 to 10 miles (13 to 16 km) with the pack | Once | If day two feels fine, you are ready for shorter Camino stages |
| 11 (already walking) | Two days running of 12 miles with the full pack | Once | The Foxes' real test: how the second morning feels |
| 12 (both) | Ease off: two or three shorter walks, then rest | 2 to 3 times | Arrive fresh, not tired |

## If it goes wrong

- **Blisters.** Stop and cover a hot spot as soon as you feel it. Change socks or shoes now, not on the trail.
- **Sore knees on descents.** Shorten your stride downhill, slow down, and consider walking poles.
- **The second day is a struggle.** Repeat that week rather than adding distance. On the Camino itself, plan shorter stages and a rest day each week, as the Foxes do.
- **Pain that does not ease, chest pain or severe breathlessness:** stop. Call 999 (911 in the US) for chest pain that spreads or does not go away.

## Track it

Write down each walk: date, distance, time, pack weight and how your feet felt. Note how you felt on each second day. By week 11 the second-day notes tell you what daily distance suits you.

## What next

Choose a route that matches your second-day distance. A shorter section is a fine first Camino; the Foxes went back for other routes: the Primitivo, the Portugués and the Invierno.
