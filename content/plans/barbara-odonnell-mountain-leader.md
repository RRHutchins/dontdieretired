## Why this works

Barbara O'Donnell did not become a mountain leader by deciding to. She became one by logging hikes: 40 of them, she told The Echo in Cork, in all weathers, often at night, and never the same route twice. Then came two assessments, and a pass in the month she turned 50.

The leader qualifications are built on a logbook. Mountain Training, which runs the UK scheme, asks for 20 "quality mountain days" before its six-day Mountain Leader training course and 40 before the five-day assessment, with eight nights camping. Mountaineering Ireland's route starts with a Mountain Skills assessment. So whether or not you ever lead anyone, the useful thing to do is the same: go out, navigate, write it down.

The NHS (the UK health service) lists hiking as moderate activity and recommends at least 150 minutes of that a week.

## Before you start

- **Check with your GP (family doctor)** if you have not exercised for some time or have a medical condition or concern.
- **Kit.** O'Donnell's list: good walking boots and a weatherproof jacket. Add a paper map, a compass, a head torch, food, water and a warm layer.
- **A logbook.** A notebook will do. Mountain Training has its own online one, called DLOG, for people who register.
- **Company.** For first hill days go with a club or a qualified leader. Clubs, O'Donnell says, cater for all levels.
- **Money.** Registration is £69 with Mountain Training and €76 with Mountaineering Ireland (their pages, 9 October 2026). Courses are extra and vary by provider.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 (getting going) | Join a led walk on low hills, 2 to 3 hours. | 1 led walk, 2 walks of 30–40 minutes | Ask the leader to show you your position on the map three times. |
| 1–3 (already fit) | Plan and navigate a hill day of 5 hours or more on a route new to you. | 1 hill day a fortnight, 2 shorter sessions a week | Start the log. No route twice: that was O'Donnell's rule. |
| 4–6 (getting going) | Plan a 3-hour route on marked paths and walk it with a friend, following it on your own map. | 1 planned walk, 2–3 walks of 40 minutes | Leave your route and return time with someone at home. |
| 4–6 (already fit) | Two more hill days, one in rain or low cloud on ground that is not steep or exposed. | 2 hill days | Take a compass bearing at every change of direction. Turn back if it stops being safe. |
| 7–9 (getting going) | A 4-hour hill walk with a club, checking every turn on the map. | 1 hill walk, 3 shorter | You are now past 150 minutes a week. |
| 7–9 (already fit) | Finish a familiar route after dark with a head torch and a partner. | 1 hill day, 2 strength sessions a week | Night navigation was part of O'Donnell's logged hikes. |
| 10–12 (getting going) | Lead a friend on a full day you planned, 5 hours. | Once, plus your usual walks | Then choose a next step below. |
| 10–12 (already fit) | A two-day walk with an overnight camp, in a region you have not walked before. That makes six logged days. | Once | Camping nights count towards the assessment total. |

## If it goes wrong

- **You are lost.** Stop. Go back to the last place you were sure of.
- **The weather turns.** Shorten the route. The hill will be there next week.
- **Sore knees on descents.** Shorten your stride, use poles, and halve the next outing. Pain that is sharp or lasts needs a GP or physiotherapist.
- **You miss a fortnight.** Pick up at the last row you finished.
- **Chest pain that spreads or does not ease, severe breathlessness or fainting:** stop and call 999 (911 in the US).

## Track it

One line per outing: date, route, hours, weather, who navigated, one decision you made. Count full hill days, regions and nights camped. If you later register for a qualification, check the scheme's own definition of a "quality mountain day" in its handbook before you count them.

## What next

- **Ireland:** a Mountain Skills course and assessment, the first step Mountaineering Ireland lists towards Mountain Leader.
- **UK:** join a mountaineering council, register with Mountain Training, and book Mountain Leader training once you have 20 days logged.
- **US:** look at a hiking club's leader path. The Appalachian Mountain Club's New York–North Jersey chapter asks for membership, two outings, a workshop and co-leading with a mentor.
