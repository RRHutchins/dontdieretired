## Why this works

Geoff Collis learned to bake during a pause in his flying career, by asking a bakery a question and then turning up. Within a matter of months he was making breads and pastries, ABC News reported. This plan follows the same idea at home: one simple loaf, repeated until your hands know it, then one change at a time.

It uses the Real Bread Campaign's basic recipe (Sustain) because it needs only four ingredients and no special kit, and its tips because they come from bakers who teach.

## Before you start

- **Kit:** a large bowl, kitchen scales, a measuring jug, a baking tray or loaf tin, a clean damp tea towel, a wire rack and oven gloves. That is all.
- **Ingredients for one loaf:** 500 g strong flour (white, wholemeal or a mix), 340 g water at about 25°C (hand-warm), 5 g dried active yeast or 10 g fresh, and 5 g salt. The Campaign suggests avoiding "instant" sachet yeast, which usually contains additives.
- **No scales?** 350 ml water, 1 teaspoon salt and 1½ teaspoons yeast.
- **Time:** about three and a half hours from mixing to cooling, most of it waiting. Pick a morning at home.
- **Hot oven:** 230°C is very hot. Use oven gloves and keep a clear space to put the tray down.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (never baked) | The basic loaf, exactly as written: mix, knead 10–15 minutes, rise until doubled (about an hour), shape, rise 40–60 minutes, bake 15 minutes at 230°C (450°F) then 30–40 at 200°C (400°F) | Once | Sticky is normal. Do not add flour. |
| 1 (already baking) | The basic loaf, then the same dough shaped and chilled in the fridge overnight before baking | Twice | Compare taste and crust side by side. |
| 2 | The same loaf again, this time shaped in a tin as a sandwich loaf, or as eight rolls (20–25 minutes in the oven) | Once or twice | Write down what you changed. |
| 3 | Swap a quarter of the white flour for wholemeal (keep the total at 500 g; add about 20 g more water as the wholemeal share rises) | Once or twice | The Campaign suggests moving to more wholemeal step by step. |
| 4 (never baked) | Bake the basic loaf from memory, recipe closed | Once | Then check it against the recipe. |
| 4 (already baking) | Slow proof plus pot: chill the shaped dough overnight, an hour at room temperature, bake in a heavy lidded pot preheated for about 20 minutes at the oven's top setting, lid off for the last stage | Twice | Lower to the recipe temperature when the lid comes off. |
| 5 | Learn from someone better: a bread class, or ask a local bakery if you can watch | Once | Ask how they judge when dough is ready. |
| 6 (never baked) | Bake two loaves and give one away | Once | You have a habit now. |
| 6 (already baking) | Bake for others: 10 to 20 loaves across the week for neighbours or a bread club | Across the week | Food hygiene certificate first if you sell anything. |

## If it goes wrong

- **Dense, heavy loaf:** usually not risen enough. Give it longer next time; the times are a guide and a cool kitchen is slower.
- **Too sticky to handle:** wet your hands and the worktop rather than adding flour, as the Campaign advises.
- **Pale crust:** your oven may run cool. Bake a little longer at the end.
- **Went stale fast:** do not keep bread in the fridge, where it stales fastest. Eat it within a day or two, or slice and freeze it.

## Track it

Keep one line per bake: the date, the flour mix, how long each rise took, the oven times and one word about the result. By week 4 you will see your own kitchen's timings, which matter more than any recipe's.

## What next

- **If you want it to be work:** the National Careers Service lists three apprenticeships of about two years (Baker, Lead Baker and Pastry Chef) and the option of starting as a trainee. A food hygiene certificate is a short course.
- **If you want it to be generous:** a weekly bread club for your street or a community fridge.
- **If you want it to be about the flour:** look for an independent or stone-grinding mill near you, which the Campaign recommends.
