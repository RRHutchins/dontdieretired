## Why this works

Karen Kennedy did not swim nearly 30 km (about 18.5 miles) in one go in her head. She thought in 30-minute windows between feeds, and, as she told the Weekend Argus, "One crossing at a time. One stroke at a time." She also had people around her: a swimming partner beside her, a support boat and, as she put it, a whole team. And she had come back to swimming after more than twenty years away, which means the habit was built, not inherited.

This plan copies those three things: small blocks, company and steady build-up. The RNLI, the UK and Ireland's lifeboat charity, recommends swimming with others and going into cold water slowly, and that is built into every week.

## Before you start

- **Talk to a health professional** before your first cold-water swim, as the RNLI advises, especially if you have a heart or breathing condition.
- **Find your people.** A sea or lake swimming group, a club, or one friend who swims. Our page on [joining a sea swimming group](/list/join-a-sea-swimming-group/) has ways in for the UK and the US.
- **Kit:** a bright swim cap, goggles, a tow float (an inflatable float you tow behind you, which makes you easy to see and gives you something to hold), a phone in a waterproof pouch, warm layers and a hot drink for afterwards. A wetsuit is optional; the RNLI says it keeps you warmer and adds buoyancy.
- **Know the water:** check the forecast and tides, swim where others swim, and tell someone on shore where you are going and when you will be back.
- **Know the emergency number:** 999 or 112 in the UK (ask for the coastguard at the coast), 911 in the US.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (getting going) | Pool: swim 10 minutes without stopping, any stroke, resting at the wall if you need to. | 3 times | Count lengths so you can see progress. |
| 1 (already swimming outdoors) | Pool or open water: 3 x 20 minutes steady, one-minute stop between blocks. | 3 times | Note distance per block; this is your baseline. |
| 2 | Getting going: 15 minutes continuous. Already swimming: 2 x 30 minutes, sipping a warm drink at the stop. | 3 times | Practise drinking from a bottle while treading water. |
| 3 | Getting going: go along to your group as a visitor; a first dip of a few minutes, walking in slowly. Already swimming: 75 minutes as 30 + 30 + 15. | 2 sea or lake sessions plus 1 pool | Splash your neck and face before you go in, and wait for your breathing to settle. |
| 4 | Getting going: 20 minutes continuous in the pool; a short dip with the group. Already swimming: 90 minutes as 3 x 30. | 3 times | Add sighting: look up at a fixed point every six strokes. |
| 5 | Getting going: swim 5 to 10 minutes in open water alongside the group, close to shore. Already swimming: 90 minutes in open water with a kayak or partner. | 3 times | Get out while you still feel good. Shivering that will not stop means the swim was too long. |
| 6 | Getting going: 25 minutes in the pool, practising breathing to both sides. Already swimming: 1 hour 45 minutes, feed every 30 minutes. | 3 times | Try different feeds; Kennedy used carbohydrate, protein, electrolytes and warm rooibos tea with honey. |
| 7 | Getting going: a longer group swim, 10 to 15 minutes, parallel to the shore. Already swimming: 2 hours, 4 x 30 minutes. | 3 times | Rest week if anything aches. |
| 8 | Getting going: a group swim you would have found daunting in week 1. Already swimming: enter or plan an organized event or crossing. | 2 to 3 times | Celebrate with the people who swam with you. |

## If it goes wrong

- **The cold takes your breath.** Stop, roll onto your back, tilt your head back with your ears in the water and breathe slowly until it passes. This is the RNLI's "Float to Live" advice. Then swim back or call for help.
- **You miss a week.** Repeat the last week you finished rather than jumping ahead.
- **Cramp.** Stop, float on your back and hold your tow float. Kennedy had leg cramps on her third crossing and kept going by telling herself to relax; in training, it is fine to stop.
- **Bad conditions.** The RNLI's rule is "If in doubt, don't go out." Swim in the pool instead.

## Track it

Keep a simple log: date, place, water temperature if posted, minutes in, distance, what you ate or drank, and how you felt an hour later. Over eight weeks, the line that matters is minutes in the water, in company, without feeling wrecked afterwards.

## What next

If you are getting going, keep swimming with your group through the seasons and build towards a short organized open water event. If you are already swimming outdoors, choose a longer event 12 to 16 weeks away and keep using 30-minute blocks.
