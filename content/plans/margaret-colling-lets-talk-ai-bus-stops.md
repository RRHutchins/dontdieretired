## Why this works

Margaret Colling, Sheffield Digital says, had never used AI before she got involved. What changed that was the People's Panel on AI in 2023: four days of hands-on learning with AI tools in small groups and one-to-one conversations with scientists. Then she passed it on: her idea put a University of Sheffield campaign on bus stops.

This plan copies that order: try it, question it, form a view, tell someone.

The gap is real. A survey of 3,513 people for the Ada Lovelace Institute and the Alan Turing Institute found that 40% had used a chatbot, but only 18% knew AI was being used in welfare assessments.

## Before you start

- **What you need.** A phone, tablet or computer with internet access, and a notebook. Every step uses the free version of a general AI assistant; public libraries have devices if you do not.
- **Pick one assistant and stay with it** for the six weeks. One is enough to learn on.
- **Two habits, from the first day.** Check what it tells you: an assistant can be confidently wrong, so confirm anything that matters with a second, independent source. Keep private details out: no bank or card numbers, passwords, National Insurance or Social Security number, or medical records.
- **This is not advice.** For health, legal or money matters, use it to prepare questions for your GP (family doctor), a solicitor (lawyer) or a regulated adviser.

## The plan

Each session is 20 to 30 minutes.

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask the assistant three questions on a subject you know thoroughly: your trade, your town, your hobby. Mark each answer right, wrong or half-right. | 2 sessions | **Complete beginner:** one question is enough. **Already using AI:** ask the same three questions of a second assistant and compare. |
| 2 | Give it a real, low-stakes job: turn rough notes into a polite letter, plan a week of meals from what is in the cupboard, summarise a long article you have read. | 3 sessions | Reply with a correction ("shorter", "less formal") rather than starting again. |
| 3 | Checking week. Ask for five facts with dates or figures on any subject. Verify each one somewhere independent. Count how many held up. | 2 sessions | Expect at least one miss. Finding it is the point. |
| 4 | Privacy week. Find the assistant's settings and read what it says about saving your conversations and using them for training. Rewrite one earlier request with the personal details removed. | 2 sessions | Unclear? Check the maker's own help page. |
| 5 | Form a view. Choose one way AI is used on people rather than by them (benefits or welfare decisions, job applications, loans, schoolwork). Read two independent sources. Write five sentences: what it does, who gains, what worries you. | 2 sessions | **Already using AI:** send them to your councillor, MP or member of Congress. |
| 6 | Pass it on. Sit with one person who has never tried AI and show them weeks 1 and 3 in fifteen minutes. | 1 session + 1 conversation | **Already using AI:** offer a 45-minute session to a group you belong to, with three live demonstrations and one mistake. |

## If it goes wrong

- **The answer is useless.** Say what you wanted instead, in a full sentence.
- **It got something wrong.** Good: log it. That is week 3 done early.
- **You typed something private.** Delete the conversation in the settings. If it was a password or card number, change the password or ring your bank.
- **It feels pointless.** Change the task, not the tool. Use it on something you have to do this week.
- **You feel behind.** Only two in five people in that survey had used one.
- **Nobody wants to hear about it.** Show, do not tell. One useful letter on a screen beats an explanation.
