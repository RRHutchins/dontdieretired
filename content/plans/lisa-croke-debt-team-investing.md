## Why this works

Lisa Croke did not begin with a clever investment. She began by finding two people to ask, an accountant and a financial coach, and by putting basic questions to them. She cleared her credit cards, then opened her first accounts. She told the Washington Post that she no longer felt shame talking about money.

That order is the plan: look, ask, clear, automate. It works because the hard part of money is rarely the arithmetic. It is opening the envelope.

Starting now is normal. The Post cites Fidelity Investments: two-thirds of women in the baby-boom generation began investing in their fifties or later. The planner Nick Booth told the paper that the regret he hears most is "I wish I would have started earlier." Four weeks from now you will have started.

**This plan is general information, not financial advice.** It will not tell you what to invest in. A regulated adviser can look at your own situation.

## Before you start

- **Time:** two sessions of 30–45 minutes a week. Put them in the diary.
- **Kit:** one notebook or one spreadsheet. Your bank and pension log-ins.
- **Free help (UK):** MoneyHelper and Pension Wise give free, impartial guidance. Citizens Advice and StepChange help with debt.
- **Free help (US):** your workplace plan's helpline; a non-profit credit counselling agency for debt. If you want paid advice, look for a fee-only adviser, which means they charge you directly and take no commission.
- **Scams:** nobody legitimate will ring you unasked about your pension or promise guaranteed returns. See our guide to [pension and investment scams](/money/pension-and-investment-scams-after-50/).
- **Keep private details private.** Never give log-ins or full account numbers to anyone who contacts you first.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (new to this) | Write down three numbers: what you owe (and each interest rate), what you have saved, and what goes into any pension or retirement plan each month. | 2 sessions | No judgement. Croke's numbers were $20,000 on cards, $70,000 in student loans and nothing saved. |
| 1 (already doing it) | List every pension, plan and account on one page, with its value and its annual charges. | 2 sessions | If you cannot find the charges, that is your first question for week 2. |
| 2 (new to this) | Ask one question of someone who knows: payroll, a free guidance service or your plan's helpline. Start with "What does my employer pay in, and am I getting all of it?" | 1 call, 1 session to write up | Croke's first questions were about employer matching and savings accounts. |
| 2 (already doing it) | Get your State Pension forecast (UK) or Social Security statement (US). Trace any lost pensions or old workplace plans. | 2 sessions | Our guides to both are linked below. |
| 3 (new to this) | Pick the debt with the highest interest rate and set a fixed extra payment to it. If your debts feel unmanageable, ring a free debt charity this week. | 1 session, 1 call | Croke cleared her cards before she invested. |
| 3 (already doing it) | Check you are getting the full employer match. In the US, if you are 50 or over, look up this year's catch-up limit and work out what one per cent more of pay would add. | 2 sessions | The IRS allows an extra $8,000 into a 401(k) in 2026 for those 50 and over, and $11,250 at ages 60 to 63. |
| 4 (new to this) | Set up one automatic payment into savings or your workplace plan on payday, however small. Book a date in three months to look at the three numbers again. | 1 session | Automatic beats heroic. Croke used an app that rounded up her purchases. |
| 4 (already doing it) | Book one hour with a regulated or fee-only adviser, or a free guidance appointment, and take your one-page list. Ask what you are paying and whether your mix still suits your timescale. | 1 appointment | Marianne Nishifue gave up picking stocks for a whole-portfolio view and found it less stressful. |

## If it goes wrong

- **The numbers are worse than you thought.** That is the plan working. A number written down can be dealt with. Ring a free debt charity before you do anything else.
- **You feel foolish asking.** Advisers hear basic questions all day. Croke's were basic, and she said asking ended the shame.
- **You cannot afford advice.** Use the free services listed above. Many employers' plans include free guidance.
- **Someone offers a quick fix.** Pressure, secrecy and guaranteed returns are the marks of a scam. Stop and check.
- **You miss a week.** Pick up where you left off. Four weeks done in eight is still done.
- **A partner will not engage.** Do your own numbers first. One clear page is a better start to that conversation than an argument.

## What next

- New to this? Repeat week 1 every three months and raise the automatic payment by a small amount each time. Already doing it? Put a yearly review in the diary and write a one-page summary a family member could follow.
