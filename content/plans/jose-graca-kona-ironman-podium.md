## Why this works

José Graca finished third of 246 in his age group at the 2026 Ironman World Championship, in 9 hours 20 minutes, on a course in the town where he lives. Nobody starts there. Every long triathlon is built from three ordinary sports, done often, joined together gradually, and tested on a course you can come back to.

The NHS (the UK health service) recommends 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week, or 150 minutes of moderate activity, plus strengthening on at least two days. The CDC, the US public health agency, gives the same weekly figures. The getting-going track below starts at about that amount and stays close to it. The already-fit track goes well beyond it, which is why it builds a little at a time and has easy weeks.

## Before you start

- **Check with your GP (family doctor)** if you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, breathing problems, or have not exercised hard for some time.
- **Pick the race first.** Getting going: a sprint or novice triathlon about 12 to 14 weeks away, ideally with a pool swim. Already fit: a half-distance race (1.9 km / 1.2 mile swim, 90 km / 56 mile ride, 21.1 km / 13.1 mile run) at about the same distance in time.
- **Kit you already have is enough to start:** goggles, any working bike with a helmet, running shoes that fit. Get the bike checked over by a bike shop before the long rides.
- **Learn the swim conditions.** If your race is in open water, find a supervised venue (a lido, a lake with lifeguards, a club session) for weeks 6 to 11. Never swim open water alone.
- **Strength twice a week,** 20 to 30 minutes: squats, lunges, step-ups, press-ups against a wall or the floor, and a plank. It counts towards the NHS and CDC strengthening guideline.

## The plan

A "brick" is a ride followed straight away by a run. It teaches your legs the change.

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 (getting going) | Swim 20 min, ride 30 min, run or run-walk 20 min, each on its own day, all at an easy pace. | Each sport once a week, plus strength twice | Easy means you can talk in sentences. |
| 1–2 (already fit) | Swim 2 km, ride 2 hours, run 50 min; plus one session each of 6 × 3 minutes harder on the bike and run. | 5–6 sessions a week | Note your time for a steady 1 km swim and a 5 km run as a baseline. |
| 3–4 (getting going) | Swim 25 min (aim for 400 m without stopping), ride 40 min, run 25 min. | Each sport once or twice a week | Rest at the pool wall as often as you need to. |
| 3–4 (already fit) | Long ride 2.5–3 hours; long run 70 min; swim 2.5 km with 10 × 100 m at race effort. | 6 sessions a week | Practise eating and drinking on the bike every 20 to 30 minutes. |
| 5 | Easy week for both tracks: cut every session by a third. | As before | Recovery is when the training sinks in. |
| 6–8 (getting going) | Add one brick a week: ride 30 min, then run 10 min. Swim 30 min. | 4–5 sessions a week | The first minutes of the run feel odd. That is normal and passes. |
| 6–8 (already fit) | Long brick: ride 3–3.5 hours, then run 20–30 min. Long run up to 1 hour 40. Swim 3 km, one session in open water. | 6 sessions a week | Ride at a pace you could hold all day. |
| 9 | Easy week for both tracks. | As before | Check your race's start times, cut-offs and kit rules. |
| 10–11 (getting going) | A practice race at home: swim 400 m, ride 20 km, run 5 km, with breaks between if you like. | Once, plus 3 easy sessions | You now know you can cover the distance. |
| 10–11 (already fit) | Race rehearsal: swim 1.9 km, then ride 90 km, then run 30 min, at race pace and with race food. | Once, plus 4 shorter sessions | The rehearsal is the test. Note what you would change. |
| 12 | Race week for both tracks: three short, easy sessions early in the week, then rest two days before. | 3 times | Lay out your kit the night before. Start slower than you want to. |

## If it goes wrong

- **Missed a week:** repeat the last week you finished. Do not try to catch up by doubling.
- **Pain that is sharp, or that gets worse as you go:** stop that sport for a few days and keep the other two. If it lasts, see your GP or a physiotherapist.
- **Always tired:** take an easy week early, and check you are eating enough on long days.
- **Panicky breathing in open water:** stop, float on your back with your ears under, and let your breathing settle before swimming on.
- **Chest pain that spreads or does not ease, severe breathlessness, fainting or signs of a stroke:** stop and call 999 (911 in the US).

## Track it

One page, four columns: date, sport, minutes or distance, and one line on how it felt. Add the brick sessions in a different colour. Each fortnight, look at the longest swim, ride and run you have done. Those three numbers should rise until week 11.

## What next

Go back to the same race next year. Graca races on a course he knows, and the same course and distance give you a time to beat that means something. If you finished a sprint, the next step is an Olympic distance (1.5 km swim, 40 km ride, 10 km run); if you finished a half, the full distance is the long ride and the long run made longer, at the same easy pace.
