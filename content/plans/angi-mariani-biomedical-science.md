## Why this works

Angi Mariani took a six-year, part-time degree in biomedical science alongside a hospital laboratory job, and much of the work was done on the train between Brighton and Ipswich, Suffolk Village Info News reported. It came down to a subject she cared about, a course that fitted, and hours found in the week she already had.

This plan copies those three parts. It tests the interest before you spend money, it makes you check the course properly, and it builds the hours before term starts, when they are easiest to find. That matters: the Learning and Work Institute found that cost was a barrier to learning for 24% of UK adults in 2025, so the plan keeps spending until last.

## Before you start

- **A notebook or a document** for one line per study session: date, how long, what you did.
- **One subject.** Not three. If you cannot choose, pick the one you would read about with nothing to gain.
- **Your old certificates,** if you have any, and dates. Admissions teams will ask.
- **A calendar.** Mark two fixed slots a week now, and any regular journey.
- **No spending yet.** Library books, free university lectures and public talks are enough for the first month.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (out of practice) | Read about your subject for 30 minutes and write three things you learned | Twice | Library or free online reading only. |
| 1 (already qualified) | Two study sessions of three hours on your subject, plus one commute or wait used for reading | Three sessions | Log the hours from day one. |
| 2 | Find two part-time courses on Discover Uni (UK) or a community college catalogue (US); note length, cost, entry rules and how teaching is delivered | Once | Check whether the course needs you on campus and how often. |
| 3 (out of practice) | Raise to two sessions of 45 minutes, plus one journey of reading | Three sessions | Same days each week. |
| 3 (already qualified) | Email two admissions teams asking, in writing, what credit or entry route your earlier study gives you | Once | Keep the replies. |
| 4 | Go to an open day, taster session or online information event for one course | Once | Ask how many hours a week students really spend. |
| 5 | Work out the money: fees, travel, books, and any loan or grant you could use | Once | Write the total down before deciding. |
| 6 (out of practice) | Two sessions of one hour plus one journey | Three sessions | Try a short piece of writing about your subject. |
| 6 (already qualified) | Keep the two three-hour sessions plus one journey; aim to pass 36 logged hours | Three sessions | If you are short, find out which slot keeps failing. |
| 7 | Talk to the people your study will affect: who covers what, which days you are away | Once | Mariani's mother made room for her two days a week. |
| 8 | Decide: apply, wait a year, or choose a shorter course first | Once | Look back at your log before you decide. |

**If laboratory work is the aim,** add one task in week 2: read NHS Health Careers' routes into healthcare science (including the medical laboratory assistant route) and check any degree's IBMS accreditation, because UK health service labs need biomedical scientists to be HCPC-registered.

## If it goes wrong

- **You missed a week:** start again at the same week, not week 1. Two missed sessions in a row usually means the slot is wrong, not you.
- **The subject stopped holding you:** good to know before you paid. Go back to week 1 with your second choice.
- **The course is too expensive:** look for a shorter certificate or a single module first, and ask the provider about loans or bursaries.
- **The hours will not fit:** fewer, longer sessions often work better than many short ones, and travel time counts.

## Track it

One line per session in your log: date, minutes, what you did, one word about how it went. At the end of each week, add up the hours. The total at week 8 is the honest answer to "do I have time for this?", which is the question every admissions tutor will be thinking about.

## What next

- **If you applied:** keep your two fixed slots going until term starts, so the habit is there on day one.
- **If you waited:** set a date to look again, and keep one session a week so the interest does not cool.
- **If you want to share what you learn:** Mariani is teaching herself animation to explain science. Writing a short talk for a local group or library is a simpler way to start.
