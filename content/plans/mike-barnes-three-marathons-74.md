## Why this works

Walk-run intervals are how most adults over 50 start running safely. Short jogs with walking breaks let heart and lungs adapt while the tendons, which adapt more slowly than muscles, catch up. The pattern here is the one used by beginner programmes worldwide, including the NHS Couch to 5K.

The other half of this plan is not about legs. People who start for a vague reason usually stop within months; people with a specific one, a name, a charity total, a date, keep going. The reason does not make running easier; it makes the difficulty smaller by comparison. So the plan asks you to choose a cause in week one and say it out loud by week three.

Parkrun is the third ingredient: free, weekly, timed, and full of walkers and people in their seventies. It gives you a date, a route and a crowd.

## Before you start

- Check with your GP first, particularly if you have heart, joint or blood pressure concerns, or have not exercised for years. Walk-running is gentle, but say what you are planning.
- Kit: cushioned trainers that fit (£40–£80; a running shop fits them free), layers, and a watch or phone to time intervals.
- Cost: parkrun is free. A charity place at a 10 km or half marathon later is usually free in exchange for a fundraising pledge.
- Where: [parkrun.org.uk](https://www.parkrun.org.uk) for your nearest 5 km (Saturday, 9 am); the NHS [Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/running-and-aerobic-exercises/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/) app for a voice in your ear; [RunTogether](https://runtogether.co.uk) for beginner groups; your charity's site for a fundraising page. (In the US, the Road Runners Club of America lists beginner programmes; JustGiving or GoFundMe for the fundraising.)

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write your reason down. Walk 4 min, jog 1 min, × 5 (25 minutes). | 3 sessions | Never run, or not since school? Walk 4, jog 30 seconds. Or simply walk 25 minutes briskly for the first fortnight, then start here. |
| 2 | Walk 3, jog 1, × 6. Register for parkrun. | 3 sessions | Jog means slow enough to talk. If you cannot, walk. |
| 3 | Walk 3, jog 2, × 5. Set up a fundraising page or tell the person you are doing this for. | 3 sessions | Tell three people. Out loud. |
| 4 | Walk 2, jog 2, × 6. Go to parkrun and walk the whole thing. | 2 sessions plus parkrun | Look at who else is walking. Note your time. |
| 5 | Walk 2, jog 3, × 5. | 2 sessions plus parkrun | Walk-run the parkrun this time, any ratio. |
| 6 | Walk 1, jog 4, × 5. | 2 sessions plus parkrun | Ask your charity for a running vest. Wear it. |
| 7 | Jog 5, walk 1, × 4. Then one 20-minute steady walk. | 2 sessions plus parkrun | Easy week for the legs. Same reason, less strain. |
| 8 | Parkrun: walk-run it with as much jogging as you can. Write down the time and the fundraising total. | 2 easy sessions plus parkrun | Then enter something with a date 4–6 months out. |

## If it goes wrong

- **Shins, knees or calves hurt.** Back off one week, walk more, and check the trainers. Pain that lasts more than a few days is a GP or physiotherapist visit, not a push-through.
- **You cannot jog for a minute.** Thirty seconds, then. Or fifteen. The ratio is a suggestion, the walking is the plan.
- **Motivation drops around week 4.** This is why the cause is chosen in week 1 and made public in week 3. Read your reason before you put your shoes on.
- **Weather, dark evenings, a cold.** A missed week is nothing. Pick up at the week you left; parkrun happens every Saturday.

## What next

- Enter a 10 km with a charity place, 4–6 months away. Most charities will send a beginners' plan and a vest.
- Join a RunTogether or parkrun-based beginners' group; running with people is what keeps it going in year two.
- Runners over 60 who last also lift. **Strong at 60** on two non-running days protects knees and hips. After a long gap, the **30-Day Restart** is the way back to week 1.
