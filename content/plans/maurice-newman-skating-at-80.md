## Why this works

Skating is a balance sport first. Every minute on wheels trains the stabilising muscles around ankles, knees and hips, and the reflexes that catch a wobble; exactly what fades with age and leads to falls. Learning to skate is learning not to fall, on purpose, with padding on.

It is low-impact once you are moving: gliding is kinder on the knees than running, and a lap of the park raises the heart rate without jarring anything.

The plan puts falling before skating: week one is on grass and carpet. People who learn to fall keep skating; people who fall badly in week one put the skates in the loft.

## Before you start

- This one needs a GP conversation if you have osteoporosis, a hip or knee replacement, a heart condition or balance problems. Say what you plan to do and listen to the answer.
- Kit: helmet, wrist guards, knee and elbow pads, every time. Hire skates for the first month.
- Cost: a pad set £25–£45; group lessons £10–£20; skate hire £5–£10; second-hand skates later, £40–£80.
- Where: [Skate Fresh](https://www.skatefresh.com) and [Citiskate](https://www.citiskate.co.uk) run adult beginner classes in London; most cities have a rink with adult beginner nights. Once you can stop, London Skaters and the Sunday Stroll run marshalled group skates with a slow group. (In the US, Skate Instructors Association lessons and your city's night skate.)
- Quad skates are steadier at first than inline. Try both.

## The plan

| Week | Do this | How often | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pads on. Learn to fall on grass or carpet: drop to knee pads, then wrist guards. Skates on, on carpet: stand, march on the spot, bend knees. | 3 sessions, 15 minutes | Never stood on wheels? This is your whole week, and possibly next week too. There is no rush. |
| 2 | Smooth flat ground, holding a rail or a friend. Duck walk, then short glides. Practise falling from a standstill on the wheels. | 3 sessions, 20 minutes | Bent knees, hands forward. Tall and stiff is how people fall backwards. |
| 3 | Beginner lesson one. Glide, scissor, first stop (heel brake or T-stop). | Lesson plus 2 practice sessions | Book the second lesson before you leave. |
| 4 | Practise stopping until it is boring. Fifty stops in a session. | 3 sessions, 20–30 minutes | Stopping is the whole safety story. Do not move on until it is reliable. |
| 5 | Lesson two. Turns, one-foot glides, skating round cones or bollards. | Lesson plus 2 practice sessions | Introduce a gentle slope only with an instructor. |
| 6 | Flat park lap: 15 minutes continuous skating. | 3 sessions | You are now exercising, not just learning. Notice the breathing. |
| 7 | Go to a rink adult night or the slow group of an organised skate as a spectator, then join for 20 minutes. | Once, plus 2 practice sessions | Talk to the oldest person there. |
| 8 | Full beginners' group skate or 30-minute park session with the group. | Once, plus 1 practice | Buy your own skates now, if you want to. |

## If it goes wrong

- **You fell and it shook you.** Go back a week. Practise the deliberate falls on grass again; the fear fades when the fall is familiar.
- **Ankles ache or collapse inwards.** Hire boots are often soft. Try a stiffer boot, tighten the top strap, and do calf raises and single-leg stands on non-skating days.
- **No lesson nearby.** A rink's adult session with a friend and a rail is next best. Watch a "how to stop" tutorial first.
- **Wet weather for a fortnight.** Rinks and sports halls exist; so do carpet drills at home. A fortnight off does not undo six weeks.

## What next

- Join the slow or "Sunday" group of an organised skate; the marshals are used to newcomers and the pace is social.
- Legs and hips do the work. **Strong at 60** on two non-skating days will make the glides longer and the wobbles fewer.
- Pick a milestone: a 5 km park loop, a charity skate, or a birthday skate with the group. Put a date on it.
