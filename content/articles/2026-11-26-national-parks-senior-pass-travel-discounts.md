For $80, you can get a pass that covers entrance fees at national parks, wildlife refuges and other federal recreation sites for the rest of your life. Several other discounts on official pages are real too, if smaller.

This is general information, not financial advice. Every price below is as listed on 26 Nov 2026 on the official page linked; prices and rules change, so check before you go.

## The America the Beautiful Senior Pass

The National Park Service's [Senior Pass page](https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/senior-pass-changes.htm) says US citizens or residents aged 62 or older are eligible. There are two versions:

- **Senior Lifetime Pass:** $80 (about £61), valid for life.
- **Senior Annual Pass:** $20 (about £15), valid for one year.

**What it covers.** The passes cover entrance fees and standard amenity (day-use) fees at federal recreation sites run by six agencies, from the National Park Service to the US Forest Service ([NPS Entrance Passes](https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm)). The Senior Pass page says that where the fee is per vehicle, the pass admits you and the passengers in a noncommercial vehicle. Where fees are per person, it admits "the passholder plus up to three additional adults," and children under 16 are always admitted free.

**The camping discount.** The NPS passes page says "the Senior Pass may provide a 50 percent discount on some amenity fees," such as camping, swimming, boat launch and specialized interpretive services. The Senior Pass page says only "some expanded amenity fees," for the passholder alone, so ask at each site.

**What it does not cover.** The Senior Pass generally does not cover or reduce special recreation permit fees or concessioner fees (for example, a lodge or tour run by a private company). Passes can't be refunded, transferred, replaced if lost, or bought as gifts, and you need photo ID to use one.

## How to buy it

- **In person.** The NPS recommends this, at over 1,000 federal recreation sites.
- **Online, by mail.** The [USGS Store](https://store.usgs.gov/lifetime-senior-pass) sells physical passes and adds a $10 (about £8) document processing fee, so the Lifetime Pass costs $90 online and the Annual Pass $30. You upload a photo of a document that proves your age and residency, such as a US driver's license, Green Card or US passport. If you need the pass within four weeks, the store suggests getting it at the first site you visit or asking for expedited shipping.
- **Digital.** The NPS says a digital pass from Recreation.gov can be used on a phone right away.

**Already have annual passes?** The USGS Store says you can trade them in person toward a Lifetime Pass, even if they have expired: one annual pass plus $60, two plus $40, three plus $20, or four at no extra cost. An old Golden Age Passport is honored too, says the NPS.

## The Access and Volunteer Passes

The **Access Pass** is free for US citizens and residents with a permanent disability. The **Volunteer Pass** is free for people who reach 250 service hours volunteering at federal recreation sites ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm)). If giving time to a park appeals, our page on how to [volunteer at a US national park](/list/volunteer-us-national-park/) has more.

## What changed for 2026

The NPS passes page says that "beginning in 2026," its listed free entrance days are for US citizens and residents only. Nonresidents aged 16 and over now pay a $100 (about £76) fee at 11 listed parks, including Yellowstone, Yosemite and Grand Canyon, unless they have an annual or America the Beautiful pass; a Non-Resident Annual pass costs $250 (about £190). The page describes no change to the Senior Pass.

## Amtrak: 10% off

Amtrak's [Senior Discount page](https://www.amtrak.com/seniors-discount) gives travelers aged 65 and over 10% off "most rail fares on most Amtrak trains." It does not apply to sleeping accommodation or "certain Amtrak Connection services," and it "may not be combinable with other discount offers." On services run jointly with VIA Rail Canada, the age is 60. Choose the "Senior" traveler type when you book, and carry proof of age on board.

Some routes go further. The [Downeaster](https://www.amtrak.com/seniors-discount-half-off-on-downeaster-train) (serving Boston and Portland, Maine) lists 50% off the full adult fare, in coach seats only; its terms say 65 and over, but the page summary says 62, so confirm. For more on rail trips, see our [guide to travelling by train](/travel/travelling-by-train-after-50/).

## State parks: three real examples

**New York.** The [Golden Park Program](https://parks.ny.gov/visit/passes/golden-park-program) gives New York residents 62 or older free vehicle access to most state parks, boat launch sites and arboretums, Monday to Friday except holidays. No application: your New York driver license or non-driver ID is the pass. A few sites, such as Walkway Over the Hudson, are excluded.

**Colorado.** The [Aspen Leaf Pass](https://cpw.state.co.us/buyapply/Pages/AspenPassInfo.aspx) costs $70 (about £53) per vehicle for 12 months, for Colorado residents 64 or older, with extra vehicles at $35. The holder must be in the vehicle, which must be Colorado-registered. It also gives $3 off a campsite per night, Sunday to Thursday.

**California.** [California State Parks](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=23800) gives people 62 or older $1 off vehicle day use and $2 off a night of family camping at most park units, though not at units run by concessionaires or local governments. The [Senior Golden Bear Pass](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=30279) costs $20 for a calendar year and covers vehicle day use for the holder and a spouse or partner at most units, in the non-peak season.

## City transit

New York's MTA runs a [Reduced-Fare program](https://www.mta.info/fares-tolls/subway-bus/reduced-fare) for anyone aged 65 or over, or with a qualifying disability (which includes receiving Medicare for a reason other than age). Subway and local bus rides cost $1.50 instead of $3. You apply in person or by mail; a Medicare card plus photo ID is one accepted ID. Check your own transit agency's site for a similar program.

## The catches

- **Discounts change.** Check the official page before you travel.
- **Ask.** Choose the right fare type or ask at the gate.
- **Residency rules.** The federal passes need US citizenship or residency, and New York's and Colorado's state programs are for residents only.
- **Proof of age.** Carry photo ID.
- **Small print.** Concession-run lodges, tours and permits usually aren't covered, and several of the programs above exclude weekends, holidays or peak times.

> **If you live in the UK**
> The [Senior Railcard](https://www.railcard.co.uk/senior/) is for anyone aged 60 or over and gives 1/3 off most train tickets. It costs £35 (about $46) for one year or £80 (about $105) for three years, as listed on 26 Nov 2026. There are time restrictions, including the morning peak in London and the South East, and a minimum fare on some tickets.
> The [National Trust](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/membership) (free entry to more than 500 places) charges £100.80 (about $132) a year for an individual adult aged 26 or over, or £168 (about $220) joint. Its [senior concession](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/membership/senior-membership) is for existing members aged 60 or over who have been members for at least the last three consecutive years: £75.60 (about $99) individual or £126 (about $165) joint.
> [English Heritage](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/join/) (unlimited visits to over 400 sites) lists a Senior membership for one adult aged 65 or over at £72 (about $94) a year, against £84 (about $110) for an Individual Adult aged 26 or over, and a Joint Senior rate for two adults 65+ at £116 (about $152). It lists no separate rate for people aged 60 to 64.
