Marie Fowler already had a bachelor's degree and two master's degrees from Maple Springs Baptist Bible College and Seminary. What she did not have was any recent experience of a classroom. She had been out of school, as she puts it, since 1959.

So when she enrolled at Howard University's School of Divinity at 80, her ambitions were modest.

> "It was never my thought that I would go beyond maybe one semester because, after all, I had been out of school since 1959."
> — Marie Fowler, WJLA

> "I didn't even know if I could retain information."

Three years later, in May 2024, she completed a Doctor of Ministry — and became the oldest doctoral graduate in Howard University's history.

## The doubt everyone has

"I didn't know if I could retain information" is the single most common reason people over 60 give us for not taking up study. It feels like a fact about the brain. It is actually a prediction — and Fowler's story is what happens when you test it rather than believe it.

The research on learning in later life is more encouraging than most people expect. Processing speed slows, yes. But vocabulary, reasoning and the ability to integrate knowledge — exactly the skills a doctorate demands — hold up well into the 80s, and the brain keeps forming new connections in response to challenge. What older learners lack is rarely capacity. It is usually confidence, and a structure that gets them into the room.

## Her reason

Fowler's motivation reached back generations. Her parents, she has said, "were born in an era when it was illegal for them to learn to read and write." A doctorate at 83 was not a hobby. It was a completion.

Her professor, Alice Ogden Bellis, described her simply: "She was the life of the party. She knew what she wanted and she came here and she did that."

## "If I can do it, why not you?"

Fowler's message to others is direct:

> "It is never too late... I am 83, which means if I can do it, why not you?"

## How to borrow her method

She did not set out to get a doctorate. She set out to survive one semester. That is the trick, and it applies to nearly everything on this site:

1. **Shrink the commitment to one term, one block of lessons, one month.** Doubt cannot argue with a small experiment.
2. **Show up.** The retention question answers itself in week three.
3. **Let the next step appear.** Fowler decided semester by semester. Three years passed.

Most adult education providers — colleges, the Open University, the University of the Third Age (U3A), local libraries — run courses that start every term. You do not need to know where it leads. You only need to know where the first class is.
