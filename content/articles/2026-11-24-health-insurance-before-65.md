Stop work before 65 and your job's health plan usually stops too, while Medicare is still some way off. Losing job coverage opens several doors, but most stay open for only 60 days, and one choice made at 65 can cost you for years.

This is general information, not financial advice. For Marketplace questions, call HealthCare.gov on 1-800-318-2596 or use its [find local help](https://www.healthcare.gov/find-assistance/) directory to reach a Navigator, a trained helper whose services are [free](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/navigator). For Medicare questions, your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers [one-on-one counseling](https://acl.gov/programs/connecting-people-services/state-health-insurance-assistance-program-ship) at [no charge](https://www.shiphelp.org/about-us); find yours at shiphelp.org or 1-877-839-2675.

## The clock starts when your coverage ends

Losing job coverage gives you a Special Enrollment Period on the Marketplace. For retirees under 65, HealthCare.gov says you can apply [any time from 60 days before and 60 days after your separation date](https://healthcare.gov/retirees/). Marketplace coverage can [start the first day of the month after](https://www.healthcare.gov/have-job-based-coverage/if-you-lose-job-based-coverage/) you lose your job-based coverage.

## COBRA: keep your work plan, pay the full price

COBRA lets you stay on your employer's group plan after you leave. The U.S. Department of Labor's [worker's guide](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/workers-guide-health-benefits-cobra) sets out the rules:

- It generally applies to private employers with 20 or more employees. Many states have "mini-COBRA" laws for smaller employers; your state insurance commissioner's office can tell you.
- It covers leaving a job for any reason other than gross misconduct, and a cut in hours.
- It usually lasts up to 18 months after a job loss; a disability can extend it to up to 29 months in total.
- "Your premium cannot exceed 102 percent of the plan's total cost of providing you with coverage." Some employers agree to cover part or all of it, for example as part of a severance package.
- You get at least 60 days to decide, and at least 45 days to make the first payment.

The coverage must be identical to what is currently available to similar active employees.

## The Marketplace: plans priced on your income

The ACA Marketplace (often called Obamacare) sells individual plans through HealthCare.gov or your state's own site. HealthCare.gov suggests you [compare the cost of COBRA](https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/index.html) with Marketplace plans first, adding that Marketplace plans "may cost less than COBRA, especially if you qualify for savings based on your income."

Those savings are based on your [expected household income for the year you want coverage](https://healthcare.gov/income-and-household-information/income/), not last year's. For early retirees, HealthCare.gov counts:

- retirement and pension income, including most IRA and 401(k) withdrawals (but not qualified distributions from a designated Roth account);
- both taxable and non-taxable Social Security;
- interest and dividends, including tax-exempt interest, and capital gains;
- any wages, self-employment income or unemployment compensation.

**Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage on HealthCare.gov** runs from November 1, 2026, to January 15, 2027, [says CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/plan-year-2027-marketplace-plans-prices-fact-sheet-0). On HealthCare.gov, enroll [by December 15](https://healthcare.gov/health-insurance-marketplace/) for coverage that starts January 1, or by January 15 for February 1. KFF notes that Open Enrollment starts November 1 "in most states"; if your state runs its own site, check its dates there.

## What changed for 2026 and 2027

The enhanced premium tax credits of recent years were temporary. As of KFF's reporting this fall:

- "In 2026, the enhanced premium tax credits expired," and people with incomes above 400% of the federal poverty level "no longer qualify for premium tax credits" ([KFF, 6 November 2026](https://kff.org/affordable-care-act/9-things-to-watch-for-the-2027-aca-open-enrollment-period/)).
- For 2027, eligibility is based on the 2026 poverty guidelines, and for households between 300% and 400% of the poverty level the required contribution is 10.22% of household income ([KFF, 28 October 2026](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/issue-brief/explaining-health-care-reform-questions-about-health/)).
- Average benchmark silver premiums for 2027 are up 13%: 11% in HealthCare.gov states and 16% in states with their own platforms (KFF, 6 November 2026).

The [2026 poverty guidelines](https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines) are $15,960 for one person and $21,640 for two in the 48 contiguous states and DC (higher in Alaska and Hawaii). By our arithmetic (not an official figure), 400% of those amounts is $63,840 for a single person and $86,560 for a couple.

One more change matters if you guess your income too low. [Beginning with tax year 2026](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/repayment-caps-eliminated-for-excess-advance-payments-of-the-premium-tax-credit), the IRS says the caps on paying back excess advance payments are gone: if you received more in advance than you were due, you must repay the entire excess. If your income turns out higher than you estimated, the difference is settled on [Form 8962](https://taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov/get-help/credits/health-care-premium-tax-credit/) with your tax return.

## Three other routes

**A spouse's job plan.** Under HIPAA, losing your job-based or COBRA coverage may open a special enrollment period in another group plan, including a spouse's. You "usually have only 30 days" to ask, [says the Department of Labor](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/workers-guide-health-benefits-cobra).

**Retiree coverage from your employer.** If you are actually enrolled in it, [you can't get premium tax credits](https://healthcare.gov/retirees/); if you are only eligible and do not enroll, you may qualify. Drop it voluntarily and you get no Special Enrollment Period: you wait for Open Enrollment.

**Medicaid.** Some states have expanded Medicaid to cover all people with household incomes [below a certain level](https://healthcare.gov/medicaid-chip/medicaid-expansion-and-you/). You can apply [any time, all year](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/your-options/), and enroll immediately if you qualify.

## The five routes side by side

| | What it costs you | How long it lasts | When to sign up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **COBRA** | Up to 102% of the plan's total cost | Usually up to 18 months after leaving a job | At least 60 days to elect; at least 45 days for the first payment |
| **Marketplace plan** | The plan's premium, less any tax credit you qualify for (generally with income of 100% to 400% of the poverty level) | Until you leave it; you can keep or change plans each Open Enrollment; savings stop once you are eligible for Medicare Part A | 60 days before to 60 days after losing job coverage, or Open Enrollment (Nov 1 to Jan 15 on HealthCare.gov) |
| **Spouse's employer plan** | Set by the spouse's employer | Set by the plan's rules | Usually within 30 days of losing your coverage |
| **Retiree coverage** | Set by your former employer; no premium tax credits while enrolled | Set by the plan; most require you to sign up for Medicare Parts A and B when eligible | Per your employer; drop it voluntarily and you wait for Marketplace Open Enrollment |
| **Medicaid** (where eligible) | Free or low-cost | Set by your state's rules | Any time of year |

## When you turn 65

CMS says [COBRA isn't considered coverage based on current employment](https://cms.gov/training-education/find-provider-type/employers-unions/top-five-medicare-enrollment), so it does not earn you the Special Enrollment Period that lets working people delay Medicare. Someone on COBRA who does not sign up for Medicare when first eligible "may have to pay a life-long late enrollment penalty," and may also face "significant bills." The Part B penalty is [10% for each full 12-month period](https://medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs/avoid-penalties) you could have signed up but did not. Once your Medicare starts, COBRA [may end](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/workers-guide-health-benefits-cobra) for you; each family member on the same COBRA has separate rights, and a spouse or dependents may be able to keep it.

On a Marketplace plan, you [can't get savings](https://healthcare.gov/medicare/changing-from-marketplace-to-medicare/) once you are eligible for Part A, and the plan does not end by itself. You can report your Medicare start date up to three months early so one ends as the other begins.

Our [Medicare guide](/money/medicare-explained/) has the enrollment windows, and our [Social Security guide](/money/when-to-claim-social-security/) explains why claiming is a separate decision.

## Questions people ask

**Can I get health insurance if I retire at 60?** Generally, yes. Losing job coverage opens a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period, and you may also have COBRA, a spouse's plan, retiree coverage or Medicaid.

**Is COBRA cheaper than Obamacare?** It depends on the plan and your income. HealthCare.gov says Marketplace plans "may cost less than COBRA," especially with income-based savings, so price both.

**Can I drop COBRA for a Marketplace plan?** During Open Enrollment, yes. Outside it, choosing to end COBRA early or stop paying for it does not give you a Special Enrollment Period, HealthCare.gov says; COBRA running out does.

**What happens to COBRA when I turn 65?** It does not protect you from Medicare's late penalties, and it may end once your Medicare starts. Check your Medicare dates with your SHIP well before your Initial Enrollment Period starts.

## This week

1. Ask your benefits office for your COBRA premium and the date your job coverage ends.
2. Estimate your household income for the coverage year, compare it with 400% of the poverty level, and preview Marketplace prices on HealthCare.gov.
3. Mark the 60-day deadlines in your calendar, and the 30-day one if a spouse has a job plan.
4. Book free help from a Navigator or other Marketplace assister, or call 1-800-318-2596, before you choose.
