Melanie Harris has been on the dancefloor since the late 1980s. About a year before the Express & Star told her story in June 2025, she decided it was time to be the one choosing the records.

Harris, who was 56 by then, is from Walsall in the West Midlands and DJs as Mom-A-Vibez. She swapped the dancefloor for the booth at 55, and in her first year she had two house mixes played on UK radio stations, played live sets around the West Midlands and won a "Deck Duel" on air.

## How it started

Part of the reason was practical.

"Going out is expensive these days," she told the paper. "I thought—why not bring the party to me?"

That first year ended with a paid booking: a set at the Mostly Jazz, Funk and Soul Festival in Moseley, Birmingham, as part of a takeover by Selextorhood. The paper described Selextorhood as a Birmingham-based community that supports under-represented genders in DJing. She was billed to play funk and Afrobeats in The Woods on the Sunday afternoon.

Her motto is "Who Says I Can't". She put the rest plainly:

> "I'm living proof that you don't need to be young or famous to start something bold."
> — Melanie Harris, to the Express & Star

## Why this matters

DJing looks like pressing play. It is not. Mixing two tracks so the join goes unnoticed means counting beats, matching tempos, and listening to two things at once, one in your headphones and one through the speakers. It is a real musical skill, and like any skill it improves with short, regular practice and someone to tell you what they hear.

Harris also had something many beginners lack: decades of listening. Anyone who has spent years on a dancefloor already knows what makes a crowd move and what empties a room. That knowledge does not go to waste when you change sides.

And she did not do it alone. A community like Selextorhood gives a new DJ what is hardest to find on your own: other people to learn from, and a first chance to play.

## How to start

You do not need your own equipment to learn. Most beginners' courses at music schools, colleges and arts centres supply the decks and mixer, and ask you to bring headphones and your music on a USB stick. Our guide to [learning to DJ](/list/learn-to-dj/) lists weekend and weekly courses in the UK and US, what they cover and what they cost as last listed. Check the current price and dates with the course before you book.

Start with your own taste. Pick the music you know best, as Harris did with funk and house, because knowing the tracks makes the mixing easier to learn. Then look for a collective, a community radio station or a friendly bar that runs open-deck or new-DJ nights, and ask for a slot.

Voices can be trained too: Brenda Willison, Newmarket's town crier, [took two Guinness records with one oyez](/connect/brenda-willison-town-crier-loudest-shout/). And Angi Mariani [went from running a music bar to a hospital lab](/think/angi-mariani-biomedical-science/).
