In 2022 Dave and Judy Fox set out from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, a small town on the French side of the Pyrenees, and walked 490 miles (about 790 km) to the cathedral city of Santiago de Compostela in north-west Spain.

It was meant to be one trip. It became a habit.

Dave Fox, 70, and Judy, 67, live in Grand Junction, Colorado. Over the past four years they have gone back to Spain again and again for other Camino routes, including the Primitivo and the Portugués, Beacon Senior News reported in June. This spring they left to walk the Camino de Invierno, the Winter Way.

## Bicycles first

Walking a long way is not new to them. After Dave survived a near-fatal aneurysm in 1981, the couple quit their jobs the following year, bought one-way tickets to Europe and spent six months travelling by bicycle. In 1988 they cycled through south-east China, along Australia's east coast and around New Zealand's South Island.

They first heard of the Camino through *The Way*, a 2010 film. Both are Catholics, and for them the walk is a pilgrimage rather than a test. "This isn't a feat of endurance for us," Judy told the paper. "We slow down to the speed of joy."

## How they train

The Foxes carry everything themselves: Dave a 30-litre pack, Judy a 22-litre one. "Since our first Camino, we've gone lighter and lighter," Judy said.

Their preparation starts three months before each trip. They are already keen hikers, but they lengthen their walks to 12 miles (19 km). A month out, they start carrying the packs they will use, and in the final weeks they hike three or four times a week, adding weight as they go.

The second day is the real test, Judy said: "It's one thing to walk 12 miles in one day. It's much harder getting up the next day and doing it again."

They once covered more than 22 miles (35 km) in a day, but they usually take one rest day a week, often in a larger town they want to see. They walk in trail runners, test every piece of kit before they go, socks included, and carry a light umbrella for both rain and sun.

## Why this matters

The Camino keeps getting busier. The Pilgrim's Reception Office in Santiago gave a Compostela, the certificate for finishing, to 530,919 people in 2025, 6% more than in 2024, according to American Pilgrims on the Camino, which publishes the figures. Half of them were 46 or older, and among Americans the share was 71%. Most (83%) arrive between May and October.

Some routes are far quieter. The Invierno runs from Ponferrada through the River Sil valley, with views over the Ribeira Sacra, and follows the way medieval pilgrims used in winter to avoid the high passes of O Cebreiro and Foncebadón. Santiago Ways, a Camino travel company, puts it at 263 km (about 163 miles) in ten stages and said in an article first published in 2018 that just 700 pilgrims had used it the previous year; Beacon Senior News described the Foxes' walk as roughly 250 miles.

## How to start

You do not need to walk 490 miles to begin. The NHS describes a brisk walk as about 3 mph (5 km/h): you can talk but could not sing. Build distance gradually, then practise walking on consecutive days, as the Foxes do.

A pilgrim credential, the passport you get stamped along the way, is needed for pilgrim hostels and for the Compostela. American Pilgrims on the Camino sends them to non-members for a $5 minimum donation; allow four weeks for US addresses and six elsewhere, or collect one at most starting points.
