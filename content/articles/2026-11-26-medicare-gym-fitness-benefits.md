A gym with a pool, a weekly class, workouts to follow at home: for people on many Medicare Advantage plans, these come with the plan at no extra cost. Finding out whether you have one takes one phone call or a few minutes online.

This is general information, not medical or financial advice. If you are starting something new or have a health condition, check with your doctor first. UnitedHealthcare's own Renew Active pages say the same: "Consult your doctor prior to beginning an exercise program." If you are new to Medicare itself, start with our [Medicare explained](/money/medicare-explained/) guide.

## The key fact: Original Medicare doesn't pay

Medicare.gov is plain about it: "Original Medicare doesn't cover gym memberships or fitness programs" ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/gym-memberships-fitness-programs)). If you have only Original Medicare (Parts A and B), you pay all costs for gym memberships and fitness programs.

The same page says Medicare Advantage Plans, other Medicare health plans and Medicare Supplement Insurance (Medigap) plans may include gym memberships or fitness programs as extra coverage, and it tells you to call your plan to see whether yours does. CMS's booklet [Understanding Medicare Advantage Plans](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/12026-understanding-medicare-advantage-plans.pdf) (April 2026) puts it this way: with a Medicare Advantage Plan, "you may have coverage for things Original Medicare doesn't cover, like fitness programs (gym memberships or discounts)."

## The programs you are likely to see

Plans that include a fitness benefit usually offer it through a named program, and that name is often what appears in your plan papers.

**SilverSneakers.** SilverSneakers is a registered trademark of Tivity Health, Inc., according to a [2025 SilverSneakers member flyer](https://trs.ky.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/SilverSneakers.pdf) published by Kentucky's Teachers' Retirement System. The program's [own site](https://www.silversneakers.com/?p=10693) describes it as included with many Medicare Advantage plans "at no additional cost to you." It lists "15,000+ participating locations" (as listed on 26 Nov 2026), live online classes, on-demand workout videos, the SilverSneakers GO app with a digital membership card, and community classes at recreation centers and parks. Its [FAQ page](https://tools.silversneakers.com/Help) adds that locations can include cardio equipment, pools, hot tubs, saunas, tennis courts and walking tracks, that you should contact each location to see what's available to you, and that you can go as little or as often as you want. The program's site says the gym membership "does not include extras like smoothies, massages, or personal training."

**Renew Active.** UnitedHealthcare says [Renew Active](https://uhc.com/member-resources/health-care-programs/wellness-and-rewards-programs/renew-active) is "available with select UnitedHealthcare Medicare Plans — at no additional cost," for use "either at a gym or from home." Its [plan page](https://www.uhc.com/medicare/shop/renew-active.html) lists a free gym membership at a gym near you plus a national gym network naming Orangetheory Fitness, Pure Barre and Club Pilates, thousands of on-demand videos and live-streamed classes, local classes and clubs (walking, hiking, dancing, gardening, bowling), AARP's Staying Sharp brain health program and an online Fitbit Community that doesn't need a Fitbit. Members use the UnitedHealthcare UCard to get into the gym, according to the [AARP Medicare Plans page](https://aarpmedicareplans.com/shop/renew-active). UnitedHealthcare adds that the program "varies by plan/area and may not be available on all plans."

**Silver&Fit.** An [American Specialty Health brochure](https://go.silverandfit.com/hubfs/ASH%20Conferences%20and%20Webinars/2024_Subsidized%20Fitness%20Network%20Webinar/S950-839I_S&F%20Program%20Overview%20Digital%20Brochure_Final.pdf) written for health plans says the program "is provided by American Specialty Health Fitness, Inc." and is offered to plans serving Medicare Advantage, Medicare Supplement and Medicaid members. It describes a fitness network of 26,000+ locations, home fitness kits (one per benefit year), live virtual events and on-demand workouts. Each plan chooses its own design, including how many gyms are at no cost to members, so what you get depends on your plan.

## How to check yours, step by step

1. **Find out what kind of coverage you have.** If you have only Original Medicare (Parts A and B), there is no gym benefit. If you also have a Medicare Advantage Plan, a Medigap policy or another Medicare health plan, that plan might include one.
2. **Read your Evidence of Coverage.** CMS describes this yearly plan document as giving "details about what the plan covers, how much you pay, and more." Look for words such as "fitness," "gym" or the program's name.
3. **Call the number on your member card.** Medicare.gov's own advice is to call your plan. Ask the questions below.
4. **Try the program's own tool.** SilverSneakers has an eligibility checker on its website. Renew Active members sign in to their UnitedHealthcare member site.
5. **Call the gym before you go.** The SilverSneakers FAQ suggests calling a location before your first visit to ask whether you need an appointment to set up your no-cost membership.

## What to ask your plan

- Does my plan include a fitness benefit, and what is the program called?
- Is the membership at no extra cost, or do I pay part?
- Which gyms near me are included, and are they all included at no cost?
- Does it cover classes, the gym floor and the pool, or only some of these?
- Can I use it at home or online if I can't get to a gym?
- Is anything changing next year?

## The catches

**Networks.** Programs work through networks of participating locations, and what each location offers varies. A gym that is in the network may still charge for extras: SilverSneakers says personal training and massages aren't covered.

**Plans change every year.** CMS says plan costs and rules "can change each year." Your Annual Notice of Change "includes any changes in coverage, costs, provider networks, service area, and more that will be effective in January." Between October 15 and December 7 each year, anyone with Medicare can join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage Plan, and from January 1 to March 31 people already in one can switch or go back to Original Medicare ([CMS](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/12026-understanding-medicare-advantage-plans.pdf)). A fitness benefit you have now isn't guaranteed next year.

**Classes versus gym access.** Programs mix gym use, classes and online workouts. Check what your nearest location actually has before counting on it.

**Don't choose a plan for the gym alone.** A plan also sets your costs and rules, such as whether you must use its network of providers; weigh the whole plan.

## What to try first

If it has been a while, start small. A gentle class is a good way in: try a [yoga class](/list/yoga-class/), [Pilates](/list/try-pilates/), [tai chi](/list/learn-tai-chi-form/) or [aqua aerobics](/list/try-aqua-aerobics/). Our [guide to getting active](/move/how-to-get-active-after-50/) has more, and the free yearly Wellness visit in our [preventive care guide](/move/medicare-free-preventive-care/) is a good moment to talk to your doctor about what suits you.

## This week

1. Find your member card and work out what coverage you have.
2. Call your plan or use the program's checker to see whether you have a fitness benefit.
3. If you do, book one class or a first visit before the year is out.

> **If you live in the UK**
> Some local councils offer cheaper or free leisure for older residents, and schemes vary from council to council. For example, [Three Rivers District Council](https://threerivers.gov.uk/services/leisure-parks-culture/sport/sport-for-adults/free-swim-over-60) offers free swimming at set sessions for residents aged 60 and over, who register in advance and show proof of residency for a free leisure card. [Stroud District Council's concession scheme](https://stroud.gov.uk/sport-leisure-parks/swimming-pools-leisure-centres/stroud-district-concession-scheme) gives discounted use of its leisure centers to people aged 60 and over, and to people receiving Pension Credit (Guaranteed Element) and some other benefits, with a free discount card. Search your own council's website for "leisure concession" or "free swimming" to see what is offered where you live.
