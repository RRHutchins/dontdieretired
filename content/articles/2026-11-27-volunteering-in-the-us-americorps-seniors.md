AmeriCorps Seniors is a US government volunteering service that places people in regular roles through local projects run by nonprofits and public bodies. Its three programs ask very different things of you: one is about children, one about neighbors who need help at home, and one is whatever your community needs. AmeriCorps says more than 143,000 volunteers are matched each year.

Everything below is as listed on 27 Nov 2026 on the official page linked. Each local project sets some of its own rules, so check with the project before you plan around a stipend or schedule. This is general information, not financial advice.

## Who can join

All three programs are open to people 55 and over. AmeriCorps says most programs carry accident, personal liability and excess automobile liability insurance for their volunteers, and that "some programs offer a modest stipend to qualified, low-income volunteers."

## The three programs side by side

| | Foster Grandparent Program | Senior Companion Program | RSVP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What you do** | Mentor students toward higher achievement; care for premature infants or children with disabilities; mentor teenagers and young mothers | Be a friend and companion to neighbors who find daily tasks hard, so they can stay in their own homes; give family caregivers a break | Local service you choose, in six focus areas: disaster services, economic opportunity, environmental stewardship, education, healthy futures, and veterans and military members |
| **Hours** | At least 260 hours a year or 5 a week; at most 2,080 a year or 40 a week | At least 260 hours a year or 5 a week; at most 2,080 a year or 40 a week | "A few hours to 40 hours per week," as you choose |
| **Stipend** | Hourly, if you meet the income test | Hourly, if you meet the income test | No hourly rate set out; AmeriCorps' RSVP page repeats only its general line that some programs offer a modest stipend to qualified, low-income volunteers |
| **Other help** | Set by the local project | Help with transportation costs; may include meals and a yearly physical exam | Set by the local project |
| **Official page** | [americorps.gov](https://americorps.gov/serve/fit-finder/americorps-seniors-foster-grandparent-program) | [americorps.gov](https://americorps.gov/partner/how-it-works/americorps-seniors-senior-companion-program) | [americorps.gov](https://americorps.gov/serve/americorps-seniors/americorps-seniors-rsvp) |

The hours come from the federal regulations for each program ([Foster Grandparents](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/2552.51), [Senior Companions](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/2551.51)), which let each sponsor set its own schedule within those limits. Senior Companion examples on AmeriCorps' page include driving people to appointments, grocery shopping and cooking meals.

## The stipend: how much, and who gets it

**The amount.** AmeriCorps announced in 2020 that stipended volunteers "will now receive $3.00 per hour of service" (about £2.30). Since a rule that took effect on 1 October 2024, local projects may add their own funds and adjust the hourly rate, so the figure varies. As an example, [Multnomah County, Oregon](https://multco.us/info/become-foster-grandparent) lists $4 an hour (about £3) for its Foster Grandparents, including approved training time.

**The income test.** The stipend is for volunteers with incomes up to 200% of the federal poverty guideline. The latest schedule we found on americorps.gov, effective 24 January 2025, sets the limit at:

| Household | 48 contiguous states | Alaska | Hawaii |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 person | $31,300 | $39,100 | $35,980 |
| 2 people | $42,300 | $52,860 | $48,640 |

In the 48 contiguous states that is about £23,900 for one person and about £32,200 for two.

Multnomah County's page gives a 2026 limit of $31,920 for one person (about £24,300) and $43,280 for two (about £33,000), so ask your project which year's figures it uses. The schedule says a spouse's income counts if you live together, food stamps are left out, and some unpaid medical costs can be deducted. Since the 2024 rule, family support you are not legally owed no longer counts as income either.

**Tax and benefits.** For Senior Companions, the federal regulation says these payments are not subject to tax, are not treated as wages, and do not reduce eligibility for any government assistance program. Multnomah County says its Foster Grandparent stipend and meal and mileage reimbursements are non-taxable and do not affect eligibility for benefits such as SNAP or rental assistance.

AmeriCorps' pages tie only the stipend to income, so you can serve whatever you earn.

## How to find a project near you

AmeriCorps' [Pathfinder](https://americorps.gov/serve/americorps-seniors/americorps-seniors-pathfinder) lists current projects. Each listing names the sponsoring organization, the program and the counties or states it covers, with contact details to view and, for some, a phone number. The projects recruit and place volunteers themselves, so the call to make is to the sponsor, not to AmeriCorps. Our page on how to [volunteer through AmeriCorps Seniors](/list/join-americorps-seniors/) has first steps.

## If none of the three fits

These directories list roles of every kind.

- **Idealist, now home to VolunteerMatch.** VolunteerMatch merged into [Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/volunteermatch) in early 2025. It lists more than 100,000 opportunities in 56 cause areas, on site and virtual. You can search within 5 to 100 miles (8 to 160 km) of home, and filter for "Done in a Day" opportunities.
- **JustServe.** [JustServe](https://www.justserve.org/content/about) is a humanitarian offering from The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and is free for everyone "regardless of their race, religion, gender." It has about 15,000 active opportunities at any time, including virtual ones.
- **Points of Light Engage.** [Engage](https://engage.pointsoflight.org/faqs) is the volunteer search from Points of Light, which calls itself the world's largest organization dedicated to increasing volunteer service.

For more ideas, our guide to [volunteering and where to start](/connect/volunteering-after-50-where-to-start/) covers skills-based roles and joining a board, and two of our activity pages are US roles with their own training: [volunteering with the Red Cross](/list/volunteer-with-the-red-cross/) and [becoming a CASA](/list/volunteer-as-a-casa/), a court-appointed advocate for a child.

## Five questions to ask a local project

1. Which of the three programs do you run, and where would I serve?
2. What schedule do you need, and can I start with fewer hours?
3. Do you pay a stipend, at what hourly rate, and which income figures do you use?
4. What do you reimburse: mileage, meals, a physical exam?
5. What training is there before I start, and is it paid time?

## FAQ

**Do AmeriCorps Seniors volunteers get paid?**
Not a wage. Foster Grandparents and Senior Companions on lower incomes can get a small hourly stipend: AmeriCorps set $3.00 an hour (about £2.30) in 2020, and projects may now add to it (Multnomah County, Oregon, lists $4, about £3).

**What is the age requirement for AmeriCorps Seniors?**
55 and over, for all three programs.

**Does the Foster Grandparent stipend affect Social Security or SNAP?**
The federal regulation for Senior Companions says these payments do not reduce eligibility for any government assistance program, and Multnomah County says its Foster Grandparent stipend does not affect benefits such as SNAP or rental assistance. Ask your project to confirm for your own benefits.

**How many hours do Foster Grandparents work?**
At least 260 hours a year or five hours a week, and no more than 40 hours a week, under the federal regulation. The local project sets the schedule within those limits.

**What is RSVP volunteering?**
The flexible one of the three: you choose how, where and when you serve, from a few hours to 40 hours a week, through a local project that places volunteers with community organizations.

> **If you live in the UK**
> A good place to start is [NCVO](https://www.ncvo.org.uk/ncvo-volunteering/why-volunteer) (the National Council for Voluntary Organisations), whose "Find a volunteering opportunity" page says local volunteer centres "are often a good place to start" and has a map to find yours. It also points to Do IT, where you can "search more than a million volunteering opportunities by interest, activity or location"; [Doit Life](https://doit.life/volunteer) says it is free to charities and the public. For a skill from your working life, NCVO lists Reach Volunteering, and for health care roles in England, NHS Volunteering.
