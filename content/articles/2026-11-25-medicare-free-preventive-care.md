Once you have Medicare Part B, you can get a check-in visit every year and a long list of screenings and shots without paying anything. The visits are a good moment to talk about what you want to do more of, whether that is walking farther, lifting your grandchildren or getting back on a bike. The trick is booking the right thing, because a free visit and a billed one can look alike.

This is general information, not medical advice. Which screenings make sense for you is a question for your doctor, and coverage questions can go to 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227; TTY 1-877-486-2048). If you are new to Medicare itself, start with our [Medicare explained](/money/medicare-explained/) guide.

## The rule that makes it free: "accepts assignment"

Medicare.gov says "You pay nothing for most preventive services if you get the services from a health care provider who accepts assignment" ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/preventive-screening-services)). Assignment is, in CMS's words, "An agreement by your doctor, other provider, or supplier to be paid directly by Medicare" ([Your Guide to Medicare Preventive Services](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/10110-your-guide-to-medicare-preventive-services.pdf)).

## The Welcome to Medicare visit: once, in your first year

You can have this visit "Once within the first 12 months you have Part B," says [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/welcome-to-medicare-preventive-visit). It is a one-time preventive check-up to review your medical history. The page lists a review of your medical and social history, information on screenings and vaccines, a check for substance use risk, a body mass index calculation, a simple vision test, a review of your risk of depression, an offer to talk about advance directives, and a written plan for the preventive services you need.

"This visit isn't a comprehensive physical exam," the page says.

## The yearly Wellness visit: every 12 months after that

Medicare covers the yearly Wellness visit "Once every 12 months." Your first one "can't take place within 12 months of your Part B enrollment," nor within 12 months of a Welcome to Medicare visit, and you don't need to have had the Welcome visit first ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/yearly-wellness-visits)).

Your provider will ask you to fill in a questionnaire called a "Health Risk Assessment." The visit includes routine measurements such as height, weight and blood pressure, a review of your medical and family history and your current prescriptions, health advice, a talk about advance directives, and a written prevention plan, such as a checklist of screenings and vaccines. There is an optional physical activity and nutrition risk assessment, worth saying yes to if you want help getting moving. Our [guide to getting active](/move/how-to-get-active-after-50/) picks up from there.

Your provider also does a cognitive assessment to look for signs of dementia. If they find a reason for concern, Medicare.gov says Medicare covers a separate, more thorough visit.

## The catch: extra tests in the same visit

The Part B deductible doesn't apply to the visit itself, but if your provider does extra tests or services during the same visit that the preventive benefit doesn't cover, you may owe coinsurance, and the Part B deductible may apply. For something Medicare doesn't cover at all, "(like a routine physical exam), you may have to pay the full amount" ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/yearly-wellness-visits)).

So if a new problem, such as a sore knee, gets examined or tested in the same appointment, that part may be billed. Medicare.gov suggests booking a separate appointment for specific health concerns. A fair question at the desk: "If we talk about anything else, will that be billed separately?"

## The three visits side by side

| | Welcome to Medicare visit | Yearly Wellness visit | Routine physical exam |
|---|---|---|---|
| **When** | Once, within your first 12 months of Part B | Once every 12 months, starting after your first 12 months of Part B | Whenever you book one |
| **What it is** | A review of your history and a written plan for preventive care | A Health Risk Assessment, measurements, a cognitive check and an updated prevention plan | A general check-up, not one of Medicare's preventive visits |
| **Is it a physical?** | No: "isn't a comprehensive physical exam" | No: "isn't a routine physical exam" | Yes |
| **Covered by Original Medicare?** | Yes | Yes | No: listed under what Original Medicare doesn't cover ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/providers-services/original-medicare/not-covered)) |
| **Your cost** | Nothing if the provider accepts assignment | Nothing if the provider accepts assignment | You may pay the full amount |

## Screenings and shots that cost nothing

Medicare.gov lists more than 20 preventive services. Here are the main ones, with how often Medicare covers them and who qualifies, from CMS's [preventive services guide](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/10110-your-guide-to-medicare-preventive-services.pdf) (May 2026) and [Medicare & You 2027](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/10050-medicare-and-you.pdf?time=1592167762955). Each costs nothing if your provider accepts assignment, except where noted below the table.

| Service | How often | Who qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| Screening mammogram | Every 12 months | Women 40 and over (a one-time baseline at 35 to 39) |
| Colonoscopy | Every 120 months, or 24 if at high risk | Adults 45 and over |
| Stool DNA or blood-based colorectal test | Every 3 years | Ages 45 to 85, no symptoms, average risk |
| Cardiovascular screening blood tests | Every 5 years | Everyone with Part B |
| Diabetes screening | Up to 2 a year | People a provider finds at risk |
| Bone mass measurement | Every 24 months | People at risk, such as some women with estrogen deficiency |
| Lung cancer screening (low-dose CT) | Once a year | Ages 50 to 77 who meet smoking-history rules, with an order |
| Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening | Once in a lifetime, with a referral | People at risk, such as men 65 to 75 who have smoked 100 or more cigarettes |
| Depression screening | Once a year, in primary care | Everyone with Part B |
| Flu shot | Once per flu season | Everyone with Part B |
| Prostate PSA blood test | Every 12 months | Men over 50 |

**Not everything is free.** If a polyp is removed during a screening colonoscopy, you pay 15% of the Medicare-approved amount for the doctor's services. A diagnostic mammogram, the digital rectal exam for prostate screening, and glaucoma screening (every 12 months for people at high risk) cost 20% after the Part B deductible. Pneumococcal shots and hepatitis B shots (for people who meet the conditions) are free with assignment, and Medicare covers COVID-19 vaccines. The shingles vaccine comes under Part D (drug coverage), which, Medicare & You 2027 says, "generally covers all other adult immunizations," including shingles, "at no cost to you."

## If you have a Medicare Advantage plan

Medicare & You 2027 says "Plans must cover all medically necessary services that Original Medicare covers," but they "can charge different out-of-pocket costs." CMS's preventive guide says your "coverage, costs, and rules might be different" and to check with your plan. Ask your plan which are free in-network.

## Questions people ask

**Does Medicare pay for an annual physical?** No. Original Medicare doesn't cover routine physical exams. It covers the yearly Wellness visit, which is a different appointment.

**Is the Medicare Annual Wellness Visit free?** Yes, if your provider accepts assignment. Extra tests or services in the same visit may cost coinsurance or the Part B deductible.

**Why did I get a bill after my wellness visit?** Medicare.gov's pages point to one reason: extra tests or services outside the preventive benefit, done in the same visit, can cost coinsurance or the deductible. Call the office and ask how it was billed, or call 1-800-MEDICARE.

## This week

1. Check your Part B start date to see which visit you are due.
2. Book it, ask whether the provider accepts assignment, and keep new problems for a separate appointment.
3. Ask for your written prevention plan, and put each due date in your calendar.

> **If you live in the UK**
> The nearest match to Medicare's Wellness visit is the free [NHS Health Check](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/nhs-health-check/) in England, "a free check-up of the health of your heart and blood vessels." It is for people aged 40 to 74 who do not already have conditions such as heart disease, diabetes, stroke or high blood pressure (people with those should be offered regular check-ups by their care team). Your GP (family doctor) practice or local council invites you every 5 years. It checks height, weight, waist, blood pressure and cholesterol, sometimes blood sugar, and gives you a score for your risk of heart and blood vessel problems over the next 10 years. Not invited? Contact your GP practice; if it doesn't offer the check, contact your local council.
