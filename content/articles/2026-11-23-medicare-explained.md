Medicare is the US government's health insurance from 65. It comes down to two decisions: how you get your coverage, and when you sign up. Some timing mistakes cost you every month for years.

This is general information, not advice. A free SHIP counselor or a licensed adviser can look at your own situation.

## The four parts in one minute

**Part A (Hospital Insurance).** CMS says about 99% of people pay no premium for it.

**Part B (Medical Insurance).** This one has a monthly premium (see the table below).

**Part D** is prescription drug coverage.

**Part C** is Medicare Advantage: private plans that, in Medicare's words, "bundle your Part A, Part B, and usually Part D coverage into one plan."

Parts A and B together are Original Medicare. [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/how-does-medicare-work) notes that with Original Medicare "there's no limit on what you'll pay out-of-pocket in a year unless you have supplemental coverage." That gap is why the next choice matters.

## What it costs for 2026, and for 2027

CMS published the [2027 figures](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2027-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles) on November 17, 2026; [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/what-does-medicare-cost) and its [drug-cost page](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/part-d/basics/costs) give both years.

| | For 2026 | For 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Part B standard monthly premium | $202.90 | $209.40 |
| Part B yearly deductible | $283 | $292 |
| Part A inpatient deductible (per benefit period) | $1,736 | $1,776 |
| Part D yearly cap on out-of-pocket spending for covered drugs | $2,100 | $2,400 |

Higher incomes pay a higher Part B premium. Part D premiums vary by plan; no drug plan may set a deductible above $615 for 2026 ($700 for 2027).

## Route one or route two

You choose Original Medicare, usually with a Medigap policy and a Part D plan, or a Medicare Advantage plan. Medigap is "extra insurance you can buy from a private insurance company" to help pay the copays, coinsurance and deductibles that Original Medicare leaves you. In most states [Medigap policies are standardized](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/basics) (Massachusetts, Minnesota and Wisconsin do it differently): plans with the same letter have the same basic benefits, and only the price differs between companies.

| | Original Medicare + Medigap (+ Part D) | Medicare Advantage |
|---|---|---|
| Doctors and hospitals | Any doctor or hospital that takes Medicare, anywhere in the US | Usually the plan's network and service area (except emergency and urgent care); you may need referrals |
| Yearly out-of-pocket limit | None in Original Medicare itself; Medigap helps pay your share | Every plan has a yearly limit on what you pay for covered Part A and B services |
| Drug coverage | A separate Part D plan (Medigap plans sold after 2005 include no drug coverage) | Most plans include Part D |
| Travel abroad | Original Medicare generally does not cover care outside the US; some Medigap plans pay 80% of emergency care abroad, up to plan limits | Plans generally don't cover care outside the US; some offer emergency cover as an extra |
| Extras | Not covered by Original Medicare | Plans may offer some vision, hearing and dental |
| Switching later | You can join Medicare Advantage at Open Enrollment | You can return to Original Medicare, but Medigap may cost more or be refused (see below) |

Sources: Medicare's [Understanding Medicare Advantage Plans](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/12026-understanding-medicare-advantage-plans.pdf) and [Choosing a Medigap Policy](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/02110-medigap-guide-health-insurance.pdf).

## The Medigap catch

Your [Medigap Open Enrollment Period](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/ready-to-buy/when) is the 6 months starting the first day of the month you are 65 or older and signed up for Part B. Medicare.gov calls it "the best time to buy."

After that, "there's no federal guarantee that an insurance company will sell you a Medigap policy." Outside that window, Medicare's Medigap guide says an insurer can use medical underwriting, meaning it can look at your health history, charge you more or turn you down, unless you have a guaranteed issue right (for example, if your Medicare Advantage plan leaves your area).

One safety net: if you join Medicare Advantage for the first time and leave within 12 months, you have a federal "trial right" to buy a Medigap policy. Some states give more rights; your SHIP will know.

## The enrollment windows

- **Initial Enrollment Period.** Seven months: the 3 months before the month you turn 65, that month, and the 3 months after. If you already get Social Security at least 4 months before 65, you are [signed up automatically](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/how-do-i-sign-up-for-medicare); otherwise, sign up through SSA.gov.
- **General Enrollment Period.** January 1 to March 31 each year, if you missed your first chance. Coverage starts the next month; penalties may apply.
- **Special Enrollment Period.** If you or your spouse are working and covered by that job's group health plan, you can [delay Part B](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65). You then have 8 months after the job or the coverage ends, whichever comes first. COBRA does not extend that window.
- **Open Enrollment.** [October 15 to December 7](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/11219-Understanding-Medicare-Advantage-Medicare-Drug-Plan-Enrollment-Periods.pdf) each year: join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage or drug plan. Changes start January 1.
- **Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment.** January 1 to March 31, for people already in a Medicare Advantage plan (and the first 3 months for new members): one change, to another plan or back to Original Medicare.
- **Medigap Open Enrollment.** Your one-off 6 months (above).

## How the late penalties are worked out

**Part B.** [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs/avoid-penalties): "You'll pay an extra 10% for each year you could have signed up for Part B, but didn't," counted in full 12-month periods. Medicare's [enrollment booklet](https://www.medicare.gov/publications/11036-enrolling-medicare-part-a-part-b.pdf) says you pay it "for as long as you have Part B." Medicare.gov's example: wait 24 months and the penalty is 20%, so for 2026 you would pay $243.50 a month instead of $202.90 ($251.30 for 2027). There is generally no penalty if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period.

**Part D.** You pay an extra 1% for each full month you go without a drug plan after first becoming eligible, or for a gap of 63 days or more without creditable coverage (drug coverage similar in value to Part D, such as some employer plans). The 1% is of the "national base beneficiary premium": $38.99 for 2026 and $41.33 for 2027. Wait 14 months and the penalty is 14%, about $5.50 a month for 2026 ($5.80 for 2027), added to your plan premium for as long as you have Medicare drug coverage. People who get Extra Help don't pay it.

## Questions people ask

**Do I have to sign up for Medicare at 65 if I'm working?** Not always. With group coverage from your or your spouse's current job, you can usually delay Part B without penalty. Check with your benefits office and SHIP first.

**Is Medigap or Medicare Advantage better?** Neither suits everyone. It depends on your doctors, prescriptions, travel and appetite for risk. The underwriting catch makes your first choice the one that counts.

**Can I switch from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare?** Yes, at either Open Enrollment, but a Medigap policy may not be guaranteed.

**What is the Part D out-of-pocket cap for 2026?** $2,100 for covered drugs; $2,400 for 2027.

## Free help

- **SHIP**, your State Health Insurance Assistance Program, gives "free, unbiased help" and is federally funded. Find yours at [shiphelp.org](https://www.shiphelp.org/what-we-do/about-ships/) or call 1-877-839-2675.
- **1-800-MEDICARE** (1-800-633-4227; TTY 1-877-486-2048), around the clock every day, except some federal holidays, according to [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/using-medicare/helpful-tools).
- **Medicare's plan finder** at [Medicare.gov/plan-compare](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare/) lets you compare Medicare Advantage and drug plans in your area and enter your prescriptions to estimate costs.

> **If you live in the UK**
> There is no Medicare equivalent to sign up for at 65. NHS (the UK health service) care is based on "clinical need, not an individual's ability to pay," and services "are free of charge, except in limited circumstances sanctioned by Parliament," says the [NHS Constitution for England](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-nhs-constitution-for-england/the-nhs-constitution-for-england). The one step it asks of you is to register with a GP (family doctor) practice.
