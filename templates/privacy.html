Privacy & cookies

Who we are. {{ cfg.site.name }} is operated by {{ cfg.company.name }} (company number {{ cfg.company.number }}, registered in {{ cfg.company.registered_in }}), registered office {{ cfg.company.registered_office }}. {{ cfg.company.name }} is responsible for the personal information described on this page.

What we collect. If you join the newsletter we store your email address and, if you chose one, your reader profile (e.g. "Restarter") with our email provider ({{ cfg.newsletter.provider|capitalize }}). We use it to send you the email and nothing else.

Analytics. {% if cfg.analytics.cloudflare_token %}We use Cloudflare Web Analytics, which sets no cookies and does not track you across sites; it counts page views, where visits come from and the type of device.{% elif cfg.analytics.plausible_script_id %}We use Plausible, a privacy-friendly analytics tool that sets no cookies and collects no personal data.{% else %}We use analytics to see which pages are read.{% endif %}

Advertising cookies. If you accept cookies, our advertising partner (Google AdSense or equivalent) may set cookies to show relevant ads. Choose "Essential only" in the banner to opt out; you can change this anytime via "Change my profile" in the footer.

Your profile. Your chosen reader profile is stored in your browser only (localStorage) so we can put relevant stories first. It never leaves your device unless you join the newsletter.

Your say (votes and suggestions). The Your say page needs no sign-in and sets no cookies. Your browser keeps a random number so you can't vote twice; we store only a scrambled (hashed) version of it with your votes and topic choices. We never store your IP address: we use a scrambled, day-by-day version of it only to stop automated floods, and it can't be traced back to you. Suggestions store the text you write and, only if you type it in, your email so we can reply. Ask us to delete a suggestion at any time.

Shared links. When someone uses a Share button, the link carries a short marker (via=friend) so we can count visits that came from a share. It identifies no one, and we never see who a link was sent to.

Your list (Things to do). The list you build, your notes, dates and your own activities are kept in your browser on your device. They are never sent to us unless you sign in to an account (see below). We count, anonymously, that a challenge was added or done (by its catalogue name), never what you wrote. An invite link carries the name of the challenge and nothing about you or your friend.

Your account (Planner Plus). When you ask for a sign-in link we keep your email address with that link until it is used or runs out (30 minutes; spent and expired links are deleted within the hour after). Using the link creates your account, which holds your email address, to sign you in and, if you join, to link your membership. We do not use it for marketing. We keep only a scrambled (hashed) copy of each link. Signing in sets one cookie, needed to keep you signed in on that device; it is not used for tracking or advertising and lasts up to 90 days. To limit abuse we count sign-in requests using scrambled versions of the address and the network they come from, deleted within about three hours. No analytics or advertising scripts run on the sign-in page. Sign-in emails are sent for us by Resend, which keeps its own delivery records. Once you are signed in, your list and plans (what you add, the dates you choose, steps you tick, places you type and any notes you write on your list) are kept with your account so they are the same on each device where you sign in; they are used for nothing else. You can delete your account at any time from Your account; it is removed from our records straight away, with the list and plans kept with it. Signing out or deleting your account leaves the copy saved in your own browser where it is.

Paying for Planner Plus. Payments are taken by Stripe on its own pages; your card details go to Stripe and never reach us. Stripe keeps your name, email address, billing address and payment records under its own privacy policy. From Stripe we keep only what we need to know whether you are a member: your Stripe customer reference and, for each subscription, whether it is active, monthly or yearly, and when the paid period ends. Deleting your account ends your subscription, deletes your customer record at Stripe and removes these from our records; Stripe keeps its own payment records.

Planner Plus gifts. When you buy a gift, Stripe takes the payment as above. We keep the gift code, scrambled (hashed) so it can be checked, and, until it is used, a locked (encrypted) copy so that you can see and print it again from your account; we also keep which account bought it, which Stripe payment it came from, and whether and when it has been used. When someone uses a code we record which account used it and start their twelve months; the buyer is told only that it has been used and when, not by whom. The names and message you type on the card stay in your browser and are never sent to us. If you delete your account, gifts you bought still work for whoever has the card, but your copy of the codes is deleted, and a gift you used stays marked as used. For each gift we keep the reference to its Stripe payment, even after the buyer's account is deleted, so that it can still be refunded and its code cancelled.

Your calendar address. If you make a calendar address on your account, we keep a scrambled (hashed) form of it so we can recognise it, never the address itself, with when it was made and the last day a calendar app read it, and a copy of the last calendar we made from your plans, so we do not rebuild it each time. When your calendar app reads the address, our server sees the request as any website does, including the network address it came from, which we do not keep; we count reads in the same way as other requests, with keyed hashes that hold no address. Anyone who has your calendar address can see your plans, so keep it to yourself; making a new one, stopping it or deleting your account makes the old one stop working at once, and deleting your account deletes all of this.
