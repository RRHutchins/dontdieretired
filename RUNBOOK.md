# Don't Die Retired — Operations Runbook

This is the instruction set Claude follows to run dontdieretired.com. It is written so a fresh Claude session with this repo can do the job without any other context.

## 1. What the site is
A static site for over-50s, encouraging physical and mental activity, run by AI with human founders. Every article is built on a real, named, linked news story. Tone: positive, plain, no hype, no medical claims, no "you must". British spelling.

**The ethos (carry it in every article):** retirement is leaving a job, not leaving life. The site's stance is that the years after 50 are the time to do MORE, not less — to make the most of life rather than resign from it. This applies equally, and is written explicitly for, people who were never physically or mentally active: beginners are the point, not an afterthought. Practically: every article should speak to both the lifelong-active reader and the never-active one (usually via the 'Try this week' box having a true beginner's option); never frame activity as a duty or use guilt; frame it as freedom and appetite; never use "despite their age" or "still" ("still running at 80"). The message is invitation, not instruction.

### 1a. House style (set 29 Sept 2026 — the model is New York Times feature writing)
**Headlines.** One line, sentence case, British spelling. Lead with the person and what they did, because it is interesting or an achievement in its own right — "Betty Brussel broke three swimming world records before lunch". The age is context, not the headline: it goes in the standfirst and the first paragraph, and appears in a headline only when it is itself the record ("oldest woman to finish…"). Never open a headline with an age ("At 83, …", "An 83-year-old…"). Never start with a pronoun ("He…", "She…"), and never use the two-sentence age formula ("She did X at 60. At 65 she…") — it was the house template for the first eleven stories and it reads as a template. Before writing a headline, read the last seven and make this one structurally different (a name, a deed, a place, a colon, a prepositional opening). Present tense for features where it fits. No "still", no "despite", no "never too old", no exclamation marks, no questions, no puns on age. The headline is the promise; the standfirst adds what the headline could not fit.

**Ledes.** Open with a scene, a fact or a detail, not a generalisation. Banned openers: "Most of us…", "Most people…", "There is a type of story…", "Not every story…". Name, age and place on first mention, Times-style: "Jeremy Lockwood, 68, a retired family doctor on the Isle of Wight, …".

**Prose.** Short paragraphs, one idea per sentence, specific numbers over adjectives. Quotes carry the story; attribute with "said" or "told [outlet]", never "enthused", "beamed", "quipped". No rhetorical questions in the first half. Second person only in the practical section and the Try-this box. Cut clichés on sight: "age is just a number", "proving that…", "inspirational", "journey", "incredible". The tone is a well-edited newspaper feature: warm because the facts are, not because the adjectives are.

Repo layout: `site.yaml` (config), `content/articles/*.md`, `content/videos.yaml`, `content/products.yaml`, `templates/`, `static/`, `build.py` → `dist/`; `social/` gets a post pack per article.

## 1b. Who we write for (set 2 Oct 2026 — read before choosing a story)
Readers are **interested, not old**. A fit 50-year-old and an unfit 75-year-old both read this site, and neither wants the other's advice. So:
- **Lead with what is interesting** (the feat, the idea, the place, the science), never with the age. Ask: would this be worth reading if the person were 35? If not, find a better story.
- **Spread the ages.** Across any two weeks at least half the people we write about should be in their 50s or early 60s. Stories about people 80+ are the exception, used when the story itself is remarkable.
- **Rotate the level** of the practical advice: about 40% `active`, 30% `advanced`, 30% `starter`. Never assume the reader is unfit. Chair-based or "ten-minute walk" advice belongs only in `starter` pieces.
- **Every article sets** `level` (starter / active / advanced / any; `any` for non-physical topics) and, if there is a person, `subject_age`.
- **Every article has two "Try this" lists**: `try_this` (for someone getting going) and `try_this_plus` (for someone already active or fit: a real stretch, with numbers). Non-physical topics get the same split as "new to this" and "already doing it". The site shows each reader the one that matches their profile.
- Readers set a profile (fitness level, age band, interests) on the site and in the app; it orders their homepage, picks their "Try this", and tags their newsletter sign-up (`level-*`, `age-*`, `int-*`).
- **Never claim a cadence we don't keep.** If the site says daily or weekly, it happens; if a job is missed, catch up the same day and say so in the report.

## 2. Daily job (runs every morning, ~06:00 UK)
1. **Find a story.** Web-search for a genuine, recent story of someone 50+ starting or achieving something (sport, study, business, creative, volunteering). Prefer 2024–2026, reputable outlets (BBC, Guardian, regional UK press, ABC AU, CBC, NYT/AP, Guinness). Fetch and read the source. Only use facts and quotes visible on the page. Rotate: women/men; **geography roughly 40% UK, 40% USA, 20% rest of world** (the US over-50 audience is ~5× the UK's and pays the highest ad rates); physical/mental; ages 50s–90s; and rotate categories (move / eat / think / money / earn / connect / travel / tech / **ai** (people doing something interesting with artificial intelligence: learning it, teaching it, building or creating with it, or defending others against its misuse; see §6b) / stories / **explore** — science, nature and the outdoors as things to DO: birdwatching, citizen science (BTO, RSPB Garden Birdwatch, iNaturalist, Zooniverse), astronomy, geology and fossil walks, wild swimming, garden rewilding, Wildlife Trust / National Trust / Audubon / Master Naturalist volunteering, U3A and OU science groups — not science news). Write for both audiences: keep British spelling, but gloss UK-only terms on first use — "GP (family doctor)", "NHS (the UK health service)" — give distances/weights in both units where they matter, and prices as £/$ when quoting costs. US-specific hooks worth using when relevant: Medicare's free SilverSneakers gym benefit, US Masters swimming/track, senior games, community colleges for learners.
2. **Write it.** Headline and prose per §1a (read the last seven headlines first; vary the structure). `python new_article.py "Title" --category X --tags a,b --segments restarter,mover --level active --age 57` (it refuses headlines that start with He/She or use the age formula). Fill front matter: `hook` (≤120 chars, used on social + image), `lesson` (one sentence), `standfirst`, `summary`, 3 `try_this` actions, `sources` with URLs. Body 450–700 words: the story, 2–3 verified quotes, a "why this matters" section with the evidence, a practical section. Link to 1–2 related articles.
2b. **Write the plan behind the story.** Create `content/plans/<slug>.md` (front matter: title, article: <slug>, summary, duration, level) with sections: Why this works / Before you start / The plan (table: Week, Do this, How often, Notes — with a true-beginner row) / If it goes wrong / Track it / What next. 500–800 words. This is the email-gated premium layer; it must exist for every article.
2c. **"How to start" guide and "Try it near you" listings.** If today's story is about an activity that has no beginner's guide yet (check `content/local.yaml` → `activities` and the `kind: guide` articles), write one: `content/articles/<date>-<activity>-for-beginners.md`, `kind: guide`, 700–1,000 words per docs/EVERGREEN_BRIEF.md, covering why it is easier than it looks, the honest challenges, the good part (the fun and the evidence), what to bring, and how to start this month. Then add the activity to `content/local.yaml` with 1–3 checked listings per region (uk, us-east, us-central, us-mountain, us-pacific, canada, elsewhere — skip a region rather than pad it). Put `local: <activity-id>` in the front matter of both the guide and the story; the story links to the guide once. Listing rules: every entry is a real place whose own or official page you fetched today (set `checked:`); prices say "from" when they vary; re-check any entry older than six months when you touch that activity; anything paid for gets `sponsored: true` (it renders a label); lesson/experience links on Viator or GetYourGuide get `aff: viator|getyourguide`, and the ids come from `site.yaml` → `affiliates`. If the activity already has a guide, just add `local:` to the story and link the guide. The reader's region comes from the browser time zone only (app.js §5): no IP lookup, no cookie, no consent needed; readers can change it with the selector.
3. **Videos: every Monday, Wednesday and Friday** add one verified YouTube film to the TOP of `content/videos.yaml` with `added: <today>`, `level` (starter/active/advanced/any) and an honest `why`. Verify the ID via `https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ID&format=json` and check the title/channel match. The page promises this cadence and shows the last-added date, so do not skip it. Spread the subjects: most films should be about people in their 50s and 60s doing interesting things (events, expeditions, new careers, skills, science), not only record-breaking 90-year-olds.
4. **Build + verify.** `python build.py`. Open `dist/<category>/<slug>/index.html`; confirm sources render, no template errors, image generated.
5. **Publish.** `git add -A && git commit -m "Daily: <title>" && git push` (GitHub Actions deploys to Pages in ~1 min).
6. **Social.** `python post_social.py social/<date>-<slug>` — schedule TWO slots per article: 08:00 UK (UK morning) and 13:00 UK (8am US Eastern) so both audiences see it fresh; Pinterest and Facebook are the US over-50 channels (needs scheduler credentials; otherwise the posts sit in `posts.md` for Zapier/manual). Image cards: `square.png` (IG/FB), `story.png` (Reels/Stories/TikTok cover), `landscape.png` (X/LinkedIn/FB link).
7. **Short video.** Send `short_video_script.md` to the video tool (HeyGen/Pictory/InVideo API) when that account exists; upload to YouTube Shorts, Reels, TikTok via the scheduler.

## 3. Weekly job (Sunday)

Same lead story chosen for interest; the "one thing to try" and the second story are picked for that level; add one line per popular `int-*` interest when there is a matching new article.
- Newsletter (send 12:00 UK Sunday = 7am US Eastern): assemble the week's best story + one "try this" + one video into the provider's editor (Buttondown/Beehiiv) and send to the free list; a longer version with the printable planner to the paid list.
- Analytics review (Plausible): top pages, entry pages, `segment` event split, `affiliate_click`, `product_click`, `newsletter_submit`, `scroll_depth`. Log the numbers in `reports/YYYY-WW.md`.
- Apply the **improvement loop** in §5.

## 5. Improvement loop (the "if traffic grows, we improve" rule)
Every Sunday, compare this week with the last four. Act on triggers:
- **Article read depth**: if `scroll_depth 90` < 40% of `scroll_depth 50` on a format → shorten, add sub-heads, move "Try this" higher. Test one change for two weeks.
- **Traffic > 1,000 sessions/week** → start the short-video pipeline properly (daily Reel/Short/TikTok from `short_video_script.md`), because video is the cheapest way to multiply reach at that stage.
- **Traffic > 5,000/week** → commission/produce one longer YouTube piece per week (5–8 min "second start" story, script written by Claude); apply to Ezoic.
- **Segment split** shows one profile > 40% → write 2 of 7 weekly articles for that segment; build the next digital product for it.
- **Affiliate clicks > 2% of article views** on a category → add a "kit list" article for that category (higher-intent, better conversion).
- **Newsletter signups < 1% of visitors** → change the lead magnet or move the form above the fold; test two weeks.
- **Product clicks but no sales** → lower the price or add a preview PDF page.
- **Your say (monthly, 1st of the month)** → export `GET /api/admin?token=ADMIN_TOKEN` (votes, interests, suggestions). Then: (1) pick the top-voted open proposal that's feasible and set it `planned` with a one-line `note` of what we'll make and when; set shipped ones `done`; (2) turn the 1–3 best new suggestions into `open` proposals (reword clearly, no names or personal details); (3) weight next month's article rotation towards the top interests; (4) tell readers in the next Sunday newsletter what won and what's coming ("You asked, we did"). Reply by email to any suggestion that left an address and needs an answer. Never publish suggestion text verbatim. No live Q&As or live events: this site is run by AI and everything is written and asynchronous.
- **"Was this for you?" (Plausible event `article_fit`)**: weekly, look at the split of yes / too basic / too much / not my thing by category and level. If "too basic" > 25% on a level, pitch that level harder; if a category draws "not my thing" > 40%, change the angle before writing more of it.
- **Any article shared > 50 times** → make a video of it and a follow-up article.

## 6a. Categories and sales focus (set 28 Sept 2026)
The site's priority is scaling DDR and increasing sales. Depth comes from the categories in site.yaml, each a hub with: one pillar "start here" guide, 5+ sourced evergreen guides (docs/EVERGREEN_BRIEF.md), at least one own product, and honest affiliate picks. Categories: move, eat, think, money, earn, connect, travel, tech, ai, explore, stories (site.yaml).
- Weekly, add one evergreen guide to whichever category has the fewest guides, linking its pillar and its product once.
- Every product in content/products.yaml must have a category and a PDF in products/pdf/.
- `products.store_live` in site.yaml: false = buttons say "Notify me" and go to the newsletter (the Gumroad store does not exist yet). Set true only once every product is live on Gumroad at its URL (permalink = product id).
- Honesty: no invented readers, testimonials, sales counts, "most popular" labels or backdated articles. Affiliate "why" lines describe what the item is for, never claims about readers.

## 6b. The AI section (added 2 Oct 2026)
Category `ai` (/ai/). Its job: readers finish better able to use AI and harder to fool with it. It follows docs/EVERGREEN_BRIEF.md → "The AI section" for sourcing, even-handedness between AI makers and the health/money/law limits. There is no AI product yet (readers can vote for an audio course on Your say, proposal `audio-ai`); do not mention one until it is in content/products.yaml.
- **Stories.** About one daily story in ten goes in `ai`: a named person aged 50+ doing something worth reading about with AI. Same rules as every story (§1a, §1b, §7), plus a plan that uses only free tools and includes the two safety habits (check what it tells you; keep private details out).
- **Keeping it current (this is the promise of the section).** AI changes faster than anything else on the site, so on the **first Monday of each month**: (1) re-fetch every source cited by the `ai` guides that is an AI maker's help, policy or privacy page or a scam-reporting route, and correct any guide whose setting name, retention period, phone number or reporting address has changed; (2) search the FTC, FBI IC3, FCC, NCSC, Report Fraud, Which? and AARP for new warnings about AI-enabled scams aimed at consumers and, if there is a new pattern, add a guide or a section and link it from the pillar; (3) check whether a widely used assistant has added or removed something a beginner would notice (a free tier, a temporary-chat mode, a training setting) and update the first-conversation and privacy guides; (4) change `date` only when the substance changed, never to look fresh, and add a line "Updated <date>: <what changed>" at the foot of the body; (5) record what was checked in `reports/ai-YYYY-MM.md`.
- **Between reviews:** if a daily run comes across a major change (a new law on fake reviews or deepfakes, a new official warning, a maker changing its training default), fix the affected guide that day and say so in the report.
- **Weekly evergreen rule (§6a) applies:** `ai` launched with one pillar and six guides. Candidates for the next ones: AI in search results and how to read an AI summary; AI features already on your phone; using AI for letters, complaints and forms; AI for family history; AI for travel planning; AI and job hunting after 50; smart speakers and voice assistants; romance scams and AI-generated profiles; what the law says about AI where you live.

## 7. Guard-rails
- Never invent facts, quotes, ages or names. If the source can't be fetched, don't run the story.
- Never give medical advice or dosing; always "check with a GP first".
- Label sponsored content; keep the affiliate disclosure on every page (it's in the footer).
- No ageist framing, no "despite their age" tone, no before/after body shaming.
- Respect the `hidden`-by-default consent banner: ads load only after consent (handled in app.js).
- Keep the same URL for an article once published (slug in front matter).
