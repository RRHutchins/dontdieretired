# Don't Die Retired — Operations Runbook

This is the instruction set Claude follows to run dontdieretired.com. It is written so a fresh Claude session with this repo can do the job without any other context.

## 1. What the site is
A static site for over-50s, encouraging physical and mental activity, run by AI with human founders. Every article is built on a real, named, linked news story. Tone: positive, plain, no hype, no medical claims, no "you must". British spelling.

**The ethos (carry it in every article):** retirement is leaving a job, not leaving life. The site's stance is that the years after 50 are the time to do MORE, not less — to make the most of life rather than resign from it. This applies equally, and is written explicitly for, people who were never physically or mentally active: beginners are the point, not an afterthought. Practically: every article should speak to both the lifelong-active reader and the never-active one (usually via the 'Try this week' box having a true beginner's option); never frame activity as a duty or use guilt; frame it as freedom and appetite; never use "despite their age" or "still" ("still running at 80"). The message is invitation, not instruction.

### 1a. House style (set 29 Sept 2026 — the model is New York Times feature writing)
**Headlines.** One line, sentence case, British spelling. Lead with the person and what they did, because it is interesting or an achievement in its own right — "Betty Brussel broke three swimming world records before lunch". The age is context, not the headline: it goes in the standfirst and the first paragraph, and appears in a headline only when it is itself the record ("oldest woman to finish…"). Never open a headline with an age ("At 83, …", "An 83-year-old…"). Never start with a pronoun ("He…", "She…"), and never use the two-sentence age formula ("She did X at 60. At 65 she…") — it was the house template for the first eleven stories and it reads as a template. Before writing a headline, read the last seven and make this one structurally different (a name, a deed, a place, a colon, a prepositional opening). Present tense for features where it fits. No "still", no "despite", no "never too old", no exclamation marks, no questions, no puns on age. The headline is the promise; the standfirst adds what the headline could not fit.

**Ledes.** Open with a scene, a fact or a detail, not a generalisation. Banned openers: "Most of us…", "Most people…", "There is a type of story…", "Not every story…". Name, age and place on first mention, Times-style: "Jeremy Lockwood, 68, a retired family doctor on the Isle of Wight, …".

**Prose.** Short paragraphs, one idea per sentence, specific numbers over adjectives. Quotes carry the story; attribute with "said" or "told [outlet]", never "enthused", "beamed", "quipped". No rhetorical questions in the first half. Second person only in the practical section and the Try-this box. Cut clichés on sight: "age is just a number", "proving that…", "inspirational", "journey", "incredible". The tone is a well-edited newspaper feature: warm because the facts are, not because the adjectives are.

## 1b. Who we write for (set 2 Oct 2026 — read before choosing a story)
Readers are **interested, not old**. A fit 50-year-old and an unfit 75-year-old both read this site, and neither wants the other's advice. So:
- **Nobody is excluded by age.** The name implies the audience; the copy never needs to say it. Do not put "over 50", "after 50", "in your 50s", "seniors" or "later in life" in headlines, standfirsts, section blurbs or product names (`new_article.py` rejects such headlines). Readers under 50 are welcome and have their own age option in the profile (`u50`). Mention age in the body only where it changes the facts (bone density, insurance rules, pension ages).
- **Lead with what is interesting** (the feat, the idea, the place, the science), never with the age. Ask: would this be worth reading if the person were 35? If not, find a better story.
- **Spread the ages.** Across any two weeks at least half the people we write about should be in their 50s or early 60s. Stories about people 80+ are the exception, used when the story itself is remarkable.
- **Rotate the level** of the practical advice: about 40% `active`, 30% `advanced`, 30% `starter`. Never assume the reader is unfit. Chair-based or "ten-minute walk" advice belongs only in `starter` pieces.
- **Every article sets** `level` (starter / active / advanced / any; `any` for non-physical topics) and, if there is a person, `subject_age`.
- **Every article has two "Try this" lists**: `try_this` (for someone getting going) and `try_this_plus` (for someone already active or fit: a real stretch, with numbers). Non-physical topics get the same split as "new to this" and "already doing it". The site shows each reader the one that matches their profile.
- Readers set a profile (fitness level, age band, interests) on the site and in the app; it orders their homepage, picks their "Try this", and tags their newsletter sign-up (`level-*`, `age-*`, `int-*`).
- **Never claim a cadence we don't keep.** If the site says daily or weekly, it happens; if a job is missed, catch up the same day and say so in the report.

## 5. Improvement loop (the "if traffic grows, we improve" rule)
Every Sunday, compare this week with the last four. Act on triggers:
- **Article read depth**: if `scroll_depth 90` < 40% of `scroll_depth 50` on a format → shorten, add sub-heads, move "Try this" higher. Test one change for two weeks.
- **Traffic > 1,000 sessions/week** → start the short-video pipeline properly (daily Reel/Short/TikTok from `short_video_script.md`), because video is the cheapest way to multiply reach at that stage.
- **Traffic > 5,000/week** → commission/produce one longer YouTube piece per week (5–8 min "second start" story, script written by Claude); apply to Ezoic.
- **Segment split** shows one profile > 40% → write 2 of 7 weekly articles for that segment; build the next digital product for it.
- **Affiliate clicks > 2% of article views** on a category → add a "kit list" article for that category (higher-intent, better conversion).
- **Newsletter signups < 1% of visitors** → change the lead magnet or move the form above the fold; test two weeks.
- **Product clicks but no sales** → lower the price or add a preview PDF page.
- **Your say (monthly, 1st of the month)** → export `GET /api/admin?token=ADMIN_TOKEN` (votes, interests, suggestions). Then: (1) pick the top-voted open proposal that's feasible and set it `planned` with a one-line `note` of what we'll make and when; set shipped ones `done`; (2) turn the 1–3 best new suggestions into `open` proposals (reword clearly, no names or personal details); (3) weight next month's article rotation towards the top interests; (4) tell readers in the next Sunday newsletter what won and what's coming ("You asked, we did"). Reply by email to any suggestion that left an address and needs an answer. Never publish suggestion text verbatim. No live Q&As or live events: this site is run by AI and everything is written and asynchronous.
- **"Was this for you?" (Plausible event `article_fit`)**: weekly, look at the split of yes / too basic / too much / not my thing by category and level. If "too basic" > 25% on a level, pitch that level harder; if a category draws "not my thing" > 40%, change the angle before writing more of it.
- **Fifty at Fifty (§6h)**: weekly, look at which challenges are added (`list_add`) and done (`list_done`) most, and write guides, plans and "Try it near you" listings for those first. Monthly, report `list_active`: it is the number that decides when accounts are worth building.
- **Any article shared > 50 times** → make a video of it and a follow-up article.

### 6i. Product sources, versions and reviews (set 7 Oct 2026)
- **Every product JSON carries** `version`, `reviewed` (month and year), `about` (how it was checked, the date sources were opened, what changed) and `sources` (group, publisher, title, year, url, supports). `build_pdf.js` renders them as the two closing sections, "About this guide" and "Where this comes from". - **A source goes in the list only if its page was opened in that session and supports the point.** Copy the title, publisher and date from the page; never from memory. Fetch one page at a time: many researchers fetching at once get the fetch service rate-limited (HTTP 429) and the run is cut short.
- **Never claim a professional review.** The "What has not happened" box stays until a named, registered professional has signed off that exact version in writing (see the Expert review brief of 30 Sept 2026). ## 6g. Daily puzzles: four a day, three levels, one streak (Wheel added 6 Oct 2026; Ladder, Sudoku, Numbers and levels added 7 Oct 2026)
`/puzzle/` has four puzzles every calendar day, the same for everyone, changing at the reader's own midnight: the **Wheel** (nine letters, every word uses the middle one), the **Ladder** (change one letter at a time), **Sudoku** and **Numbers** (reach a target with + − × ÷). They exist to give people a reason to come back each day, and Robin's brief is "variety and a challenge".
- **Levels.** The reader picks Gentle, Steady or Hard (kept in the browser, default Steady). Each level is a *different puzzle*, not the same one with more help, and a reader can mix levels across the four on the same day.

  | | Gentle | Steady | Hard |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Wheel (same wheel for all) | goal 15% of the words | goal 25% ("Good") | goal 60% ("Excellent") |
  | Ladder | 4 letters, 3 changes | 4 letters, 5 changes | 5 letters and 5 changes, or 4 letters and 7, on alternate days |
  | Sudoku | 6x6; wrong numbers show at once | 9x9, single gaps only; Check button | 9x9, needs pairs or locked candidates; Check button |
  | Numbers | 4 numbers, 2 sums | 5 numbers, 3 sums | 6 numbers, 4 or 5 sums |
- **How they are built.** `content/puzzle_days.json` holds the Wheel's nine-letter word and middle letter per date (`tools/build_puzzles.py`; answers are worked out at build time from `tools/wordlist/words.txt`, SCOWL, minus `blocklist.txt`). `content/puzzle_extra.json` holds the other three at all three levels per date (`tools/build_daily.py`, standard library only, seeded by date). `build.py` re-checks each day's puzzles with plain code and writes one small file per day to `dist/puzzle/d/`, only for two days back and fourteen ahead, plus `w4.txt` and `w5.txt` (every word a reader may use as a ladder rung). So an old puzzle really is gone, and the site must be built at least once a fortnight (the daily job does this).
- **Every puzzle is proved before it is used.** `python tools/build_daily.py --check` re-proves all of them with separate code: each ladder is a shortest route through the whole word list, each sudoku has exactly one solution and needs exactly the techniques its level says, each numbers target cannot be reached in fewer sums than its level says. Run it after generating and before committing. Never describe the puzzles as checked unless that run passed.
- **Never change a day that is already in either file**: people may be part-way through it. To stop a word being a Wheel answer or a ladder rung from now on, add it to `tools/wordlist/blocklist.txt` (ladders also honour the puzzle books' `_BLOCK` list); that only affects days generated afterwards.

### 6l. Films and photographs on stories (added 7 Oct 2026, Robin: "more photos and videos")
Stories were illustration-only. They may now carry a film and a photograph, under these rules. Both are optional and both fail safe: if anything is missing or unconfirmed the page simply shows the artwork and no film.

**Film of the person (front matter `video: {id, title, channel}`).**

- Confirm it the way §2 step 3 does: WebFetch `https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ID&format=json` and copy `title` and `author_name` exactly into `title` and `channel`. Never type an ID from memory.
- **It must be this person.** Where the title does not name them, the channel plus the particulars in the title (age, place, the feat) must match our sources. If that is not certain, leave the film off and say so in the report. (Left off on 7 Oct 2026 for this reason: a RunThrough film titled "Mike Barnes" that could not be tied to our Mike Barnes.) - A film that suits the videos page as well can be in both places.

**Photograph (front matter `photo_file`, `photo_caption`, `photo_alt`, `photo_checked`; optional `photo_label`).**
- **What it may show.** The place where the story happened, the thing the story is about, or the activity. A photograph of the actual person is used only if it is on Commons under an open licence or the owner has given written permission. Never a photograph of some other identifiable person standing in for them: prefer pictures with no people, people far off, or hands and equipment only.
- **The caption says what the picture shows** (place, and the year if it matters), taken from the file's own description on Commons, and claims nothing the description and our sources do not support. Do not write "where she trains" unless a source says so. Where people are visible, add "The picture is not of <name>." `photo_alt` describes what can be seen, for readers who cannot see it.
- **How to get one.** The cloud session cannot reach Wikimedia, so GitHub Actions does it (`.github/workflows/photos.yml`, `tools/photos.py`):
  1. Put search phrases in `tools/photo_queries.yaml` (`<slug>: ["phrase", …]`) and push. The workflow writes the candidates (title, size, author, licence, description, a small preview each) to the **`photo-research` branch**, which is never deployed. Fetch it (`git fetch --depth 1 origin "+refs/heads/photo-research:refs/remotes/origin/photo-research"`), read `candidates.json` and look at the previews.
  2. Put the chosen file's exact Commons title in the article as `photo_file: "File:….jpg"`, with `photo_caption` and `photo_alt`, and push. The workflow asks the Commons API for the author and licence, refuses anything that is not public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA (or that has no author to credit), downloads the file, saves `static/photos/<slug>.jpg` (1600px wide) and `<slug>-800.jpg`, and commits them with `content/photo_credits.yaml` and `docs/IMAGE_CREDITS.md`. Read the job's "Photos" annotation for anything refused.
  3. `git pull`, **open the downloaded file and look at it** (the Read tool shows images). Check it is what the caption says, that nobody is identifiable who should not be, and that there is no advertising, logo or artwork filling the frame. Correct the alt text to what you actually see. Only then set `photo_checked: <today>` and push. The build shows no photo without that date.
  4. Empty `tools/photo_queries.yaml` (leave the comment lines) when the choice is made.
- **Credit.** The author, the licence (linked) and "via Wikimedia Commons" (linked to the file's page) print under the picture, and again on `/photo-credits/`, which the build writes from `content/photo_credits.yaml`. Never edit that file or `docs/IMAGE_CREDITS.md` by hand.

That keeps a share-alike licence simple: we are showing the photographer's work, credited, not making a new one.

### 6r. The guidance engine (Robin, 27 Oct 2026: "consolidate the best guidance… rank it by professionalism… look for the best-ranked and most common guidance… make the output safe, include maintaining the body")

- **"Picked for you"** (home page, from `/index.json`) never repeats the hero. Newness weighs as much as interests: a story from today or yesterday gets +6, within three days +3, within a week +1, on top of a newest-first rank bonus. Stories this browser has already opened (`ddr_seen`, article paths only, kept on the device, last 300) drop by 8, so the list changes as the reader reads. Cards from the last day that the reader has not opened carry a "New" tag.
- **Topic pages** reorder with the same score (cards carry `data-date` and `data-url`).
- **`/stories/` (Real Stories) lists every daily story from every topic, newest first**, not only the few filed under `stories`.
- If you change the scoring, check with a saved profile that today's story is still first on the home page and near the top of "Picked for you" the morning after a new one is published.

## 7. Guard-rails
- Never invent facts, quotes, ages or names. If the source can't be fetched, don't run the story.
- Never give medical advice or dosing; always "check with a GP first".
- Label sponsored content; keep the affiliate disclosure on every page (it's in the footer).
- No ageist framing, no "despite their age" tone, no before/after body shaming.
- Respect the `hidden`-by-default consent banner: ads load only after consent (handled in app.js).
- Keep the same URL for an article once published (slug in front matter).

### 7a. Copyright (set 2 Oct 2026 — applies to every article, guide, plan, product, image and social post)
- **Write it yourself.** Facts are free to report; another writer's sentences are not. Read the source, then tell the story in our own words and our own structure. Never paste or lightly reword a passage, and never follow a source paragraph by paragraph.
- **Quote people, sparingly.** At most three or four short quotes per story, each under about 30 words, and no more than 15 per cent of the body in total. Every quote is attributed to the speaker and the outlet, and the outlet is linked in `sources`. Prefer what the person said over the journalist's own prose; paraphrase the journalist. `build.py` prints a COPYRIGHT WARNING when a story goes over these limits: fix it before publishing.
- **Never reproduce** song lyrics, poems, book passages, or more than a line or two of any article, even with credit. Name the work and describe it instead.
- **Story artwork (set 4 Oct 2026).** Each article gets two images at build time: the headline card (`static/img/<slug>.png`, used for social and link previews) and the on-site artwork (`static/art/<slug>.svg`, from `art.py`): the logo's sun in the topic colour, rising over a scene picked from the slug, headline and tags. After writing an article, look at its artwork in `dist/static/art/`; if the scene does not suit the story, set `art:` in the front matter to one of: sea, hills, mountains, road, rails, books, city, houses, fields, forest, cliffs, grid, steps. Avoid the same scene twice running on the homepage. A photograph replaces the artwork only under the rules in §6l.
- **Images (changed 7 Oct 2026, Robin).** Three kinds only: (1) images made by `build.py`, the product builder or our own brand files; (2) photographs from Wikimedia Commons under a public-domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA licence, fetched and credited by `tools/photos.py` exactly as §6l sets out; (3) a photograph whose owner has given us written permission (keep the email; set `photo:`, `photo_credit:`, `photo_alt:`, `photo_caption:`). Never copy a photograph, screenshot, chart, logo or illustration from a news site, social media or an image search, whatever the credit. Never use an AI-generated picture of a person or a place on the site; the one exception is the short story videos, which follow §6m. Stock-library photos of models are not used: they read as the person in the story.
- **Video.** Embed with the official YouTube player only (as `videos.yaml` and the story films in §6l do), from the channel of whoever made the film. Never download, re-upload, clip or screenshot someone else's film. Blurbs are our own words.
- **Fonts and code.** Fraunces and Source Sans 3 are used under the SIL Open Font License; keep the OFL text files beside the font files wherever they are stored. Check the licence before adding any other font, icon set or library.
- **Names and brands.** Mention organisations and products by name only to refer to them. Never use another organisation's logo, or imply that a person or organisation in a story endorses the site or its products.
- **If someone complains.** Any request to remove or correct something goes to Robin the same day; take the item down first if the claim looks plausible, then sort it out.
