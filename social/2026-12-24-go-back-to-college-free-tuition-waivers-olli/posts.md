Several US states let older residents take public college classes tuition-free. In Ohio, Ohio State's Program 60 lets residents 60 and over audit courses without tuition. In Texas, Texas State University lets people 65 and over audit free, or take up to six credit hours a semester without tuition.

The catches are real: many programs are space-available, so you may register after paying students, and fees for labs, parking or applications are often charged.

If you want classes without exams or grades, an Osher Lifelong Learning Institute (there are 124 at US colleges, in all 50 states) runs courses for people 50 and over for a membership fee.

Our guide compares eight states side by side, with the questions to ask a registrar. What would you study first?
