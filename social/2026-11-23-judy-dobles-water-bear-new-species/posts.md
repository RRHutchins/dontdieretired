Judy Dobles took some moss from a log and looked at it under her microscope.

Among the usual tiny life in it, she saw something different. It was a water bear, also called a tardigrade: an eight-legged animal, most of them well under a millimeter long (less than a twenty-fifth of an inch). This one did not look like any she had seen before.

A scientist in Poland checked its genes and its body. It was a new species, a kind of animal nobody had ever recorded.

Judy worked in finance at Kodak, the photography company. She is a longtime volunteer and citizen scientist at Cumming Nature Center in Naples, New York, which is run by the Rochester Museum & Science Center.

Over two years she sampled more than 140 sites on the center's land, and the number of water bear species recorded in New York State rose from 12 to 27.

You can look for water bears yourself, with moss, a little water and a microscope. Our story says how to start.
