Start Social Security at 62 and, if your full retirement age (the age when you get 100% of your benefit) is 67, you get 70% of your full benefit for life. Wait until 70 and it is 124%, according to the Social Security Administration (SSA).

Our guide sets out what each age pays and what a spouse can get: up to 50% of the worker's benefit at full retirement age, with no increase if the worker waits longer. It also covers what a surviving spouse can get, and the earnings test, the rule that lets SSA hold back some benefits if you claim before full retirement age and keep working. For 2027, if you are under full retirement age all year, SSA holds back $1 for every $2 you earn above $25,560.

The right month depends on your health, your work, your savings and your spouse, and money held back under the earnings test is not lost. Have you looked at your own estimates for 62, full retirement age and 70?
