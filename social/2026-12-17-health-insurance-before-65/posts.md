If you retire before 65 and lose job-based health coverage, HealthCare.gov says you can generally apply for a Marketplace plan any time from 60 days before to 60 days after your separation date. COBRA, if your employer's plan is covered, usually lets you keep your work plan for up to 18 months after leaving a job (longer in some cases), but the premium can be up to 102% of the plan's total cost, says the U.S. Department of Labor.

For 2027 coverage the enhanced premium tax credits have ended. KFF, a nonpartisan health research group, says people with incomes above 400% of the federal poverty level no longer qualify for premium tax credits.

One trap at 65: CMS, the agency that runs Medicare, says COBRA is not coverage based on current employment, so someone on COBRA who does not sign up for Medicare when first eligible may have to pay a lifelong late enrollment penalty.

Our guide compares COBRA, the Marketplace, a spouse's plan, retiree coverage and Medicaid on cost, length and deadlines. On HealthCare.gov, Open Enrollment for 2027 runs until January 15, 2027, for coverage starting February 1; if your state runs its own site, check its dates there.
