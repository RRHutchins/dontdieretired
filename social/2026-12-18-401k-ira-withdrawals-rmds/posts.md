Money in a traditional IRA can't stay there forever. The IRS says required minimum distributions generally begin at age 73, and you can delay the first one until April 1 of the following year, with the second due by December 31 that same year.

Miss one and the shortfall "may be subject to an excise tax of 25%," reduced to 10% if it is corrected within two years, says the IRS. You can ask for the tax to be waived for reasonable error by filing Form 5329 with a letter of explanation.

Roth IRAs have no RMDs during the owner's lifetime. And from age 70½, a qualified charitable distribution, paid directly from an IRA to a qualified charity, can count toward your RMD: the IRS limit is $111,000 for 2026, rising to $114,000 for 2027.
